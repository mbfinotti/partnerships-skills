**Short answer: your resourcing call is right, but "dead last on value-per-effort" is the wrong justification, and stating it that way will bite you later.**

**1. Check the capacity math first**
Five partners × 3–4 manager-months = 15–20 manager-months. Your total budget is 14. You already can't fund all five — this was never a clean "rank six, take the top N" exercise. Orrin was never going to compete for a slot against partners it can't even be compared to on the same axis.

**2. Orrin isn't low-value — it's blocked**
Value-per-effort implicitly assumes effort is the only thing standing between you and the value. That's false here: Orrin's streaming endpoint doesn't exist until Q3 next year. Your 9 manager-months can't produce anything until they ship, no matter how you rank it. That's a scheduling gate, not a value signal. Scoring it "dead last" makes a capability the CPO called your biggest gap look like a bad bet, when it's actually just not startable yet.

**3. Don't merge two different decisions into one ranked list**

- _This quarter's execution slate_: allocate the 14 manager-months across the partners that fit and ship — that's a legitimate value/effort call, and Orrin correctly has zero claim on this budget right now.
- _The strategic bet_: Orrin is the only candidate that closes the stated gap. It belongs on a separate track, timed to Q3 next year, not folded into this quarter's ranking as "worst option."

**4. What "dead last" is likely to cost you**
If Orrin sits at the bottom of a single value/effort list, it will get re-deprioritized every quarter by the same logic, forever — the ML gap never closes and nobody owns that as a decision, it just erodes silently. That's the actual risk to surface to your CPO: not "we ranked Orrin low," but "closing the ML gap is structurally deferred to next year because of a vendor dependency outside our control."

**5. What I'd do instead of a flat ranking**

- Fund the partner subset that fits in 14 manager-months this quarter, chosen on real value/effort among _those five_.
- Carve out a small discovery slice now (well under 1 manager-month) to lock the pipeline contract/schema with Orrin jointly, so your 9 manager-months can start the moment their endpoint ships — not months after.
- Get a firm commitment (not just a roadmap line) from Orrin on the Q3 date. A single-vendor, unshipped dependency is now the critical path for your company's #1 stated capability gap — that's worth naming explicitly to the CPO, not burying inside a ranking.

So: keep Orrin out of this quarter's budget — that part of your instinct is correct. But don't call it "dead last on value." Call it "highest strategic value, gated to Q3 by a dependency we don't control," and make sure someone owns re-surfacing it when that gate opens.
