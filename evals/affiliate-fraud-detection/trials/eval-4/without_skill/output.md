Two of the three things you've already done or are about to do work against you here. Walk through this before you delete anything.

**Stop the deletion for now.** Deleting the account this afternoon destroys the evidence you'd need — order history, click/session logs, IP and device data, timestamps, cookie attribution — both for the network's investigation and for clawing back commissions already paid. Suspend or freeze the affiliate account (block new clicks, hold pending payouts) instead of deleting it. Export/archive everything first if deletion is still required for other reasons.

**The network will not "pick it up from here" on its own.** Big affiliate networks (CJ, Impact, Awin, Rakuten, PartnerStack, etc.) don't passively monitor your chargeback data — they act on formal fraud reports the merchant files through their portal, with evidence attached. If you don't open a case, nothing happens on their end. Do that today:

- File a fraud/compliance case with the specific network, attaching order IDs, dates, chargeback reasons, and your reasoning for suspecting fraud.
- Ask them explicitly whether they can claw back commissions already paid on the disputed orders, and whether they'll flag/ban the affiliate network-wide (most networks share fraud blacklists, but again, only if reported).

**Diagnose what kind of fraud this actually is** — it changes who else needs to be involved:

- If chargebacks are "item not received"/"unauthorized" disputes, this could be stolen-card fraud run through the affiliate's traffic to earn commissions on orders that were never going to be paid for. That's a payments problem, not just an affiliate one — loop in your payment processor's fraud/risk team, pull AVS/CVV match data on those orders, and check whether this affiliate's chargebacks are pushing your own merchant account toward card-network chargeback-monitoring thresholds (Visa VDMP / Mastercard ECM) — that's a risk to you independent of the affiliate relationship.
- If it's cookie-stuffing, incentivized traffic, or self-referrals, that's squarely the network's compliance remit — give them the click-fraud pattern data.

**Fix the email problem.** You've already put "committing affiliate fraud" in writing to him before a network or processor has confirmed anything. If chargebacks later turn out to have an innocent explanation (a payment gateway issue, a promo abused by end customers, not the affiliate), that email is a defamation/breach-of-contract exposure. Don't send anything further like that without legal sign-off; keep future comms factual ("suspending pending investigation of anomalous chargeback activity").

**GDPR angle, since you're in France:** don't delete his account/personal data yet — fraud investigation is a legitimate-interest basis to retain data (Art. 6(1)(f)), and destroying it while an accusation is on record could hurt you if he disputes the termination. Retain, then delete on your normal schedule once the case is closed.
