## Short answer: yes, eat both — but don't just delete the problem, paper it

### Order 88412 ($148.80 commission on a $1,240 order, 12% flat)

The platform refusing to let you edit that record isn't a bug you need to route around — it's doing its job. Paid commission records should never be edited in place; that destroys the audit trail. The correct fix for a paid-in-error commission is always a separate reversing/adjusting entry, not a rewrite of history. So "I can't touch the record" is not actually a blocker.

The real question is whether you have a lever to recover it: does this affiliate have other active orders/future payouts you could net against? If yes, that's a different action ("offset a future payout") than "clawing back a paid one," and may or may not be allowed depending on how literally your terms read ("paid commissions cannot be taken back under any circumstances" plausibly also blocks offsetting, since the effect is identical from the affiliate's side). If there's no live balance to net against, don't chase it — $148.80 isn't worth a contract dispute.

Log it as a return-driven commission write-off, not a silent loss.

### PineRidge Deals ($3,110 paid in January on since-returned orders)

Eat it, and don't attempt collection. Three independent reasons converge here:

1. **Your own terms bar it.** You wrote "once paid, never taken back, no exceptions." If you now invoice or demand it back, you're the one breaching the agreement — and as the drafting party, any ambiguity gets read against you, not her.
2. **There's nothing to net.** Zero balance, no future commissions, no relationship left to leverage. This isn't a collections problem with a soft option — it's already a bad debt.
3. **The economics don't support chasing it.** She's stopped promoting you. Trying to claw back money you explicitly promised was hers-to-keep, from someone who no longer needs you, buys you legal exposure and a bad story in affiliate circles, for a sum that's a rounding error next to that risk.

### What "eat it" should actually look like operationally

- **Don't skip the paper trail because no one can co-sign this cycle.** Write up both as a return-driven commission write-off now — amount, order/affiliate, root cause, date — and route it for finance sign-off whenever someone's free. "Nobody to co-sign" is a reason to defer approval, not a reason to have no record. A write-off with a delayed signature is normal; a write-off with no documentation at all is the thing that gets questioned in an audit.
- **Combined exposure this cycle: $3,258.80.** Track it as a metric (commission paid vs. commission clawed back by refunds), not a one-off incident. If this keeps recurring, it's a signal your flat 12% rate or category mix isn't pricing in return risk, not a signal to fight individual affiliates for refunds.

### The actual fix is upstream, not retroactive

Order 88412 shows the real gap: commission locked Feb 10, refund landed Feb 19, payout still went out Feb 20 — a full day where a pre-payout refund check would have caught it for free. The fix isn't recovering this $148.80, it's inserting a hold/validation step between "commission locked" and "payout released" long enough to catch refunds that land inside your normal return window (commonly 14–30 days) before money leaves, not after. That's what turns future Wrenfield-style cases into a non-event instead of a write-off.
