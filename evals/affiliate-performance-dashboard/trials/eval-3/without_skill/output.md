### Notes per metric

- **ROAS (14:1 → 15.5:1):** You're already beating the quoted network average by a wide margin. A jump straight to industry-average+50% territory implies diminishing returns are unlikely — a ~10% YoY lift is a stretch-but-sane target, not a re-anchor to the "12:1" figure, which tells you nothing about your ceiling.
- **Active-affiliate rate (22% → 28%):** The "10–30% typical" range is too wide to be a useful benchmark (3x spread) — it's not telling you where you stand, just that you're not an outlier. The real lever is the 78% dormant affiliates: prune dead signups, reactivate lapsed ones. Target the top of the published range, not the midpoint.
- **Revenue concentration (64%, quoted 71%):** Don't treat "71%" as something to chase — a _lower_ top-10% share means revenue is less dependent on a handful of partners, which is the safer position. Set the target as a **ceiling** (stay ≤65%) to guard against concentration risk creeping up, not a floor to climb toward.
- **Reversal rate (6% → ≤5%):** No comparison figure was given, so there's nothing to validate this one against — treat it as an internal-only target until you get a sourced benchmark.

### One flag before you lock these in

The "network average," and especially the "71% figure that gets quoted everywhere," both smell like numbers repeated across blog posts without a traceable source or shared methodology (attribution window, last-click vs. multi-touch, self-reported by whom). I'd treat both as directional color, not as calibration points — the targets above are set off your own trend, using the quoted figures only as sanity checks, not anchors.
