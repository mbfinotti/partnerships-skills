## Framework: AARRR ("Pirate Metrics")

Named by Dave McClure (500 Startups, 2007). Widely recognized, so your VP can drop the name in her own conversations. It maps a funnel to five stages — perfect fit for affiliate reporting since clicks → signups → revenue is exactly that funnel.

**Acquisition → Activation → Retention → Referral → Revenue**

## KPI set mapped to the framework

| AARRR Stage | KPI                       | Target    |
| ----------- | ------------------------- | --------- |
| Acquisition | Total clicks              | 1M/month  |
| Activation  | Affiliate signups         | 200/month |
| Revenue     | Gross commissions earned  | —         |
| Retention   | _(not currently tracked)_ | —         |
| Referral    | _(not currently tracked)_ | —         |

## One thing to flag before you present it

Your three current KPIs cover 3 of 5 stages, and all three are raw volume — none of them measure efficiency or quality. That's a gap a VP who knows the framework by name will likely spot immediately (asking "what's your conversion rate?" is the obvious follow-up to Pirate Metrics). Two cheap additions make the deck defensible:

- **Click → Signup conversion rate** (signups / clicks) — turns two vanity numbers into one efficiency number.
- **Net commissions** (gross commissions minus clawbacks/reversals) — gross alone overstates the number if you have any refund/fraud window.

If you want to look further ahead, "Retention" (repeat/active affiliate rate) and "Referral" (affiliate-recruits-affiliate rate) are the two natural next KPIs to introduce once the pipeline above is running — they close out the framework and give you a built-in "here's what we'll add next quarter" talking point.
