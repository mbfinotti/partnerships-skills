## Bottom line

Don't set it at 20%. At Cablewright's margins, 20% of order value isn't a competitive commission — it's a loss on every single affiliate-referred sale. The number your economics actually support is **6% of order value (~$10.80 on your $180 AOV)**. That's not a weak offer: it sits at the top of the consumer-electronics affiliate range, and the pitch below explains why it out-earns a skincare affiliate's 20% in real dollars per sale.

## Why 20% breaks the economics

Walking your numbers through the standard ecommerce ceiling derivation:

```
AOV                                  $180.00
Gross margin (24%)                    $43.20
Variable costs (ship/pick-pack/fees) -$16.00   → contribution margin $27.20
Expected returns (7%)                  ×0.93   → post-return contribution margin $25.30
Pay 1/3 to 1/2 of that                         → $8.43 to $12.65 per order
As commission rate                             → 4.7% to 7.0% of AOV
Sanity check: 20-30% of gross margin ($43.20)  → $8.64 to $12.96 (overlaps ✓)
```

Both checks agree: the sustainable band is **~5-7% of order value**.

Now check 20%: that's **$36.00 per order**. Your post-return contribution margin is $25.30. Commission alone ($36) plus variable costs ($16) is $52 against $43.20 of gross margin — you'd be **$8.80 underwater on gross margin alone**, before ad spend, overhead, or the 7% of orders that come back as returns (where you'd have paid a commission on revenue that no longer exists). This isn't a thin margin — it's a guaranteed loss on every affiliate-driven sale, and it gets worse, not better, as affiliates get good at sending you volume.

The skincare programs you were comparing against typically run 55-75%+ gross margin on consumables. Their 20% and your 20% are not the same instrument — you're pricing a 24%-margin hardware business against a formulation business's margin structure. Independently, the industry benchmark for consumer electronics/accessories affiliate rates is 1-10% (thin-margin category), so 6-7% isn't a low offer inside your category — it's a top-of-category one.

## How to pitch it

The real fix for "affiliates take us seriously" isn't the percentage - it's what an affiliate actually earns per sale and how fast they get paid. Lead with dollars, not rate:

- **Reframe from % to $ per conversion.** A skincare affiliate on 20% of a typical $35-50 AOV nets $7-10 per sale. You're offering $10.80 per sale at 6%. Put that comparison directly in front of prospective partners - a lower headline number that pays more per click is the actual argument, not an apology for the rate.
- **Lead with category context.** State plainly that consumer electronics/accessories affiliate programs run 1-10% industry-wide because of margin structure, and that you're pricing at the top of that band, not the bottom.
- **Win on payout speed, not just rate.** Net-30 with a $50 threshold and a 30-day lock beats slower-paying competitors — Riemer's industry data shows affiliates default to whoever pays fastest when the rate is close. Say this explicitly in the pitch.
- **Court your top partners individually, off the public card.** The 5-10% of affiliates who'll drive most of your volume respond better to a milestone bonus, a longer cookie window, or a private rate than to a higher blanket percentage that would sink your margin across the whole base. Reserve a custom deal for named tech reviewers/YouTubers instead of raising everyone to 20%.
- **Don't confuse a rate problem with a recruiting problem.** Most affiliate programs stall on partner activation and outreach, not commission size. If prospective affiliates are lukewarm, a media kit, sample-unit seeding for reviewers, and direct outreach to relevant tech/deal-site partners will move the needle faster than raising a rate your margin can't sustain past the first return cycle.
