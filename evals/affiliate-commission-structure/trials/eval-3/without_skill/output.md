## Cratefox affiliate commission — 12-month vs. lifetime

**Base unit economics** (assumptions: $129/mo, 61% gross margin, 7.5% monthly churn)

- Expected customer lifetime = 1/0.075 = **13.3 months**
- Expected lifetime revenue = 129 × 13.3 = **$1,720**
- Expected lifetime gross profit = 1,720 × 0.61 = **$1,049**
- Expected revenue *within a 12-month window* (accounting for churn before month 12) = 129 × 8.10 = **$1,045**
- Key ratio: a 12-month-capped plan captures only **60.8%** of the expected months a lifetime plan captures (8.10 vs. 13.3 months) — this ratio is what lets you price the two plans to cost the same.

Reality check on "lifetime" tail risk: churn is high enough (60.6% annualized) that the tail is thin — only **~15%** of customers are still active at 24 months, **~6%** at 36 months, **<1%** at 60 months. "Lifetime" sounds unbounded but in practice pays out over ~13 months on average, same as any other cohort math.

### The two options, priced to equal cost

| | **Option A — 12-month cap** | **Option B — Lifetime** |
|---|---|---|
| Commission rate | 25% of monthly revenue | 15% of monthly revenue |
| Duration | First 12 payments only | Every month, no cap |
| Expected payout / referred customer | **$261** | **$258** |
| % of lifetime gross profit consumed | 24.9% | 24.6% |
| Net margin while commission is live | 36% | 46% |
| Net margin after commission stops | 61% (from month 13 on) | never stops — stays 46% |
| Max exposure on a customer who never churns | $387 (hard cap) | Uncapped in theory; ~$1,161 for a rare 5-year customer (still 46% margin) |
| Headline affiliate appeal | Ordinary — every competitor caps at 12 | Matches what the two competitors already advertise |
| Affiliate incentive alignment | None beyond the first year — affiliate has no reason to care about retention past month 12 | Affiliate keeps earning only if the customer stays, which self-selects for affiliates sending durable customers |

Both cost you the same in expectation (~$260/customer, ~25% of lifetime gross profit, ~15% of lifetime revenue). The difference is entirely optics, cash-flow timing, and incentive alignment — not true cost.

### If you want more room to be aggressive on the lifetime headline

Equal-EV rate pairs (12-month rate → lifetime-equivalent rate, using the 0.608 ratio):

| 12-month rate | EV/customer | Lifetime-equivalent rate | EV/customer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | $209 | 12.2% | $210 |
| 25% | $261 | 15.2% | $261 |
| 30% | $314 | 18.2% | $313 |

If you want the lifetime number to *beat* the two competitors on paper rather than just match your own 12-month plan's cost, going to **18–20% lifetime** costs $310–$344/customer (29.5–32.8% of lifetime gross profit) — still leaves 41–43% net margin every month, and is the tier I'd use if the goal is explicitly to win the three affiliates back, not just plug the leak.

### Recommendation

Publish the **lifetime option at 15%**. It costs the same as a 25%-for-12-months plan, matches the "lifetime recurring" language your competitors are using (which is what actually triggered the defections, not the raw %), and its uncapped framing costs you almost nothing extra given the churn profile. Keep 25%/12mo in your back pocket only if you want to run both simultaneously as a choice at signup — but if you're picking one to publish, lifetime wins on every axis except a slightly lower headline percentage, and "lifetime" is the word that was actually costing you affiliates.
