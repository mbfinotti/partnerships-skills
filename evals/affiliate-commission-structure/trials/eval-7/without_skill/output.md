# Announcement Email

**Subject:** Important update to your commission structure — effective Monday, September 14

---

Hi [Affiliate Name],

We're writing to let you know about a change to how commissions are calculated in the Verdantly affiliate program, effective **Monday, September 14, 2026**.

**What's changing**

Since launch, every affiliate has earned a flat 14% commission on order value, regardless of site type. Starting Monday, we're moving to a two-tier structure based on how your site drives traffic:

| Site type | New commission |
|---|---|
| Cashback, deal, and coupon sites | 7% of order value |
| Content, review, and editorial sites | 12% of order value |

**Why we're making this change**

A flat rate across every kind of traffic source doesn't reflect the different value each brings us. Content sites — reviews, guides, comparisons, gardening tutorials — introduce new customers to Verdantly and build trust before a purchase decision is made. Cashback and deal sites primarily capture demand from shoppers who were already going to buy, often at the final step of checkout. We're rebalancing payouts to better reward the traffic that grows our customer base, while keeping cashback and deal placements — which remain a valuable channel — commercially sustainable for us long-term.

**How your site was classified**

Your account has been reviewed and assigned to one of the two tiers based on your primary traffic model (e.g., presence of coupon codes, cashback offers, or deal-listing pages vs. editorial/content-led placements). You can see your assigned tier and new rate now in your affiliate dashboard under **Account → Commission Terms**.

**Think your site is misclassified?**

If your site has both content and deal/cashback elements, or you believe the assigned tier doesn't reflect how you actually drive traffic, reply to this email or open a ticket by **[date]**. We will review any classification dispute submitted within that window before your new rate is enforced, and we'll honor the resolution retroactively if a change is warranted.

**What stays the same**

- Your tracking links, cookie duration, and reporting dashboard are unaffected.
- Commissions on orders placed before Monday, September 14 at 00:00 [timezone] will be paid at your existing 14% rate.
- Payout schedules and minimum thresholds are unchanged.

We know a rate change is never welcome news, and we don't take it lightly given how much you've contributed to the program's growth. If you have questions, our affiliate team is available at [affiliate-support email] and standing by for calls this week.

Thank you for being part of the Verdantly affiliate program.

[Name]
Affiliate Program Manager, Verdantly

# Rollout Plan

## ⚠️ Timeline risk — flagging before the plan

Today is Saturday, September 12. "Next Monday" is September 14 — **two calendar days out, one of them a weekend day**. For a base of 340 affiliates, several of whom are likely earning meaningful revenue from Verdantly today, this is very short notice for a change that cuts the majority segment's rate by half. Realistic risks at this timeline:

- No time for affiliates to react, ask questions, or pause campaigns before the new rate hits — support ticket spike Monday morning.
- No time for you to audit tier classifications carefully, so miscategorized sites (common in the cashback/content gray zone — e.g., a blog with an embedded coupon widget) go out wrong in the first blast.
- No buffer to catch technical issues (dashboard not reflecting new rates, tracking rule not deployed) before affiliates see it live.

If there's any flexibility, a 5–7 day notice window is standard practice for commission changes and meaningfully reduces churn and complaint volume. If Monday is fixed for business reasons, proceed with the plan below, but treat the first 72 hours post-launch as a high-touch support window, not routine ops.

## Phase 1 — Classification (before the email goes out)

1. **Pull the full 340-affiliate list** with site URL, current 30/60/90-day order volume, and revenue contribution.
2. **Classify each site** into `cashback/deal` or `content` using consistent criteria: presence of coupon/promo-code listings, cashback payout mechanics, or deal-aggregation pages → cashback/deal; editorial, review, tutorial, or influencer content with no transactional mechanic → content. Flag mixed-model sites explicitly rather than forcing a bucket.
3. **Spot-check the top 20 affiliates by revenue** manually — a misclassification here has outsized impact and outsized complaint risk.
4. **Load the new rates into the affiliate platform** so each account's dashboard reflects the correct tier and rate the moment the email sends — do not send the email before the backend is updated, or affiliates will see stale rates and lose trust in the announcement.

## Phase 2 — Internal readiness (same day, before send)

1. Brief the affiliate support team on the change, the classification logic, and the dispute process so they can answer tickets consistently from hour one.
2. Prepare an internal FAQ / talking points doc for support to reference (why the change, how classification works, what happens on disputed cases, whether any grandfathering applies).
3. Confirm the platform correctly applies 14% to orders placed before the cutover and the new tiered rate after — test this in staging if possible.
4. Decide and document the dispute-resolution window and who owns adjudicating tier disputes.

## Phase 3 — Announcement (send Monday, or as early as classification/backend work allows)

1. Send the single email blast to all 340 affiliates (draft above).
2. Post the same information as a pinned notice in the affiliate dashboard/portal, so it's visible independent of email deliverability.
3. Update the program's public terms/commission page to reflect the new structure.

## Phase 4 — Monitoring (first 2 weeks post-launch)

Track daily for the first week, then weekly:

- **Support ticket volume and sentiment**, especially disputes over tier classification.
- **Affiliate opt-outs or account deactivations**, segmented by tier — a spike among top-revenue cashback/deal affiliates is the leading indicator of real damage.
- **Click and order volume by tier**, to see whether cashback/deal affiliates throttle promotion in response to the lower rate.
- **Classification dispute queue**: volume, resolution time, and how many result in a tier change (a high override rate signals the initial classification pass was too coarse).

## Phase 5 — Contingency

- Pre-agree an internal threshold (e.g., "if top-10 revenue affiliates by GMV pause promotion, escalate for a manual retention conversation") rather than deciding reactively.
- Decide in advance whether any grandfathering (e.g., honoring 14% for existing top affiliates for a 30/60-day transition) is on the table if churn is severe — easier to hold that in reserve than to reverse a firm "effective Monday" announcement publicly.
- Keep the dispute window genuinely open past the stated deadline for the first two weeks — a rigid cutoff during a rate cut this large tends to generate the most vocal complaints, which is a reputational risk on public affiliate forums even when defensible on the merits.
