# Maison Lumo × Camille Ferrand — compliance exposure and fixes

Not formal legal advice — treat this as a compliance brief; get French counsel (DGCCRF-facing) and, given her domicile, a quick Belgian check before you touch the contract.

## The footer line does not cover what you think it covers

You're conflating two separate obligations under the *loi du 9 juin 2023 visant à encadrer l'influence commerciale* (amending the Code de la consommation). They have different triggers and different fixes:

1. **Gift disclosure** — Camille received a €95 unit for free to review. That alone makes the *entire review* commercial content, independent of any links. The law requires a clear, legible, identifiable **"Publicité" / "Collaboration commerciale"** marker wherever the commercial content appears — not buried, not implied. Nothing on the page currently says this review is sponsored/gifted. **This is the bigger gap and the footer line does nothing for it.**
2. **Affiliate link disclosure** — for links that generate a commission, the law expects the commercial-link mention attached to (or immediately next to) the link itself, so the reader knows *which* links are tracked. A single generic sentence ("Certains liens sont affiliés") dropped at the very bottom of the page, requiring a scroll past the whole article and every buy button, fails the "clair, lisible et identifiable" standard — regulators and the self-regulatory body (ARPP) have both flagged exactly this pattern (generic, delayed, below-the-fold disclosure) as non-compliant.

So the current page has **zero valid disclosure of the sponsorship**, and **weak, arguably invalid disclosure of the affiliation**.

## Exposure

- **Deceptive commercial practice (pratique commerciale trompeuse)** under the Code de la consommation: fines up to €300,000 for an individual, up to 10% of average annual turnover for a company (can rise to 50% of the spend on the offending campaign), plus up to 2 years' imprisonment for the individual. This liability is **shared between the influencer and the advertiser** — the 2023 law was specifically written to stop brands hiding behind "that's her content, not ours."
- **You (Maison Lumo) are the easier enforcement target, not Camille.** DGCCRF acts on French advertisers directly. Going after a Brussels-domiciled creator cross-border requires the EU Consumer Protection Cooperation network (Reg. 2017/2394) with Belgian FPS Economy — slower, but not out of reach, especially since her content demonstrably targets French readers.
- **Jurisdictional pile-up, not just France:**
  - *Audience in France* → French rules apply (targeting test, not her domicile).
  - *Audience in the Netherlands* → Dutch ACM's influencer marketing guidance (same "clear at first glance" standard, generally via #ad-style near-link disclosure) is a second, independent exposure if a meaningful share of clicks/conversions come from NL readers.
  - *Her domicile, Brussels* → Belgian rules on commercial communications (FPS Economy / JEP) apply to her as the service provider; a Belgian regulator could act on her directly regardless of what French law says.
  - None of these regimes treat one footer sentence as sufficient.
- **No written contract on file (implied by the setup)**: the 2023 law requires a written contract between brand and influencer above a compensation threshold set by decree, and best practice treats *any* paid/gifted/affiliate relationship as needing one regardless of amount — it's your main lever to force her to fix the page and to protect you if she doesn't. Absent that contract, you have no documented basis to require her to correct anything, and no indemnity if she refuses.
- **It's live and ranking** — this is not a one-off Instagram story that expires. It's an indexed, durable asset. DGCCRF sweeps specifically target this profile: home/lifestyle blogs, SEO-strong, affiliate-monetized, gifted product. The longer it stays as-is, the longer the exposure window and the harder to argue "we didn't know."

## What to change

1. **Get the post edited now**, not the next campaign. Ask Camille (or have your affiliate manager ask) to add, near the top of the article, a clear "Collaboration commerciale — produit reçu gratuitement de Maison Lumo" (or equivalent), visible without scrolling.
2. **Move affiliate disclosure to each buy button**, e.g. "lien affilié" tag next to every tracked link, not one sentence at the bottom.
3. **Drop the footer line or keep it only as a redundant reminder** — it doesn't satisfy either obligation on its own and shouldn't be your only fix.
4. **Paper it retroactively**: get a short written agreement with Camille now covering (a) mandatory disclosure wording and placement, (b) your right to request corrections, (c) her acknowledgment that the €95 unit was compensation. This is required above the legal threshold anyway, and here it also creates your defense file if DGCCRF asks "what did you do to ensure compliance."
5. **Set a standard disclosure template for your affiliate program going forward** — don't rely on each creator's own footer conventions. Bake the "Publicité/Collaboration commerciale" + per-link "lien affilié" wording into your affiliate onboarding so this doesn't repeat with the next partner.
6. **Screenshot/archive the current (non-compliant) state** before it's edited, dated — you'll want it if you ever need to show remediation timeline to a regulator.
7. Given the NL audience share, ask her to phrase the top-of-page disclosure in a way an average reader immediately understands as "sponsored/gifted" even skimming — French wording is fine for FR readers, but if NL traffic is significant, don't rely on translation quality of a browser plugin to save you.

Priority order: fix #1 and #2 on the live page this week — that closes most of the acute exposure. The contract (#4) and template (#5) are what stop this from being a recurring problem with every future affiliate.
