**Neither option is sufficient on its own. Here's why, and what actually fixes it.**

## The legal exposure

This isn't a gray area — it's a squarely established FTC Endorsement Guides violation:

- **Material connection = must be disclosed.** A $4,000 flat payment is a material connection, full stop. Doesn't matter that it wasn't "sponsored content" in a formal sense — money changed hands for the video to exist.
- **"Clear and conspicuous," not buried.** FTC guidance (and multiple 2023–2024 enforcement actions) says a disclosure must be unavoidable: on-screen text during the video, stated verbally, or in the _first few lines_ of the description before "show more." A pinned comment or a line at the bottom of a description **does not qualify** — the FTC has explicitly called out pinned/buried disclosures as inadequate in past settlements.
- **This is a pattern, not an incident.** Four videos, one still trending, all missing disclosure. That's not "we forgot once" — that's a systemic failure, which matters for both regulatory risk (aggravating factor) and platform policy (YouTube's own paid-promotion rules).
- **Liability sits on both sides.** The FTC can act against Priya _and_ against Northvale Audio as the brand that paid for and benefited from the undisclosed endorsement. "She didn't disclose" is not a defense for you.

## Why your plan (as written) doesn't clear the bar

A pinned comment + a line added to the description text:

1. Doesn't fix the **video itself** — no on-screen or verbal disclosure, and viewers who don't expand the description or read comments (most people) never see it.
2. A pinned comment can be a weak fallback for _future_ posts under YouTube's Creator disclosure tools, but regulators have treated it as insufficient as the _sole_ disclosure for past content, especially when it's added retroactively after 140k views already accrued without one.
3. It doesn't create a record that you _addressed_ the compliance gap — it just patches text, five days after 140k people already watched it undisclosed.

## Why "just terminate her" doesn't clear the bar either

Firing her doesn't un-disclose anything. The four videos stay up, un-fixed, one still trending — the exposure continues to run _after_ you knew about it and chose not to fix it. That's arguably worse than doing nothing, because it demonstrates you identified the problem and prioritized punishing the messenger over remediating the harm.

## What to actually do

1. **Fix the content, properly, on all four videos:**
   - Add `#ad` at the very start of the title or description (visible without expanding), _and_
   - Add on-screen text disclosure via YouTube's editor (you can overlay text on an already-published video) stating it was a paid partnership, for at least the video's full duration or per FTC "clear and conspicuous throughout" guidance — a single frame at the start is weak.
   - Use YouTube's built-in "Paid promotion" toggle on all four — this triggers YouTube's own disclosure banner, which is more defensible than a pinned comment.
   - Pin a comment too, as a _supplement_, not the fix.
2. **Loop in legal/compliance counsel now** — not because this email needs it, but because you have a live, repeating FTC exposure across multiple assets and a trending video amplifying it in real time. They'll tell you whether proactive disclosure to the FTC or a compliance memo is warranted given the trending video's visibility.
3. **Fix the process, separately from the personnel decision:** put disclosure terms in the contract/SOW for every paid creator going forward, with a checklist step before any paid video ships — right now nothing caught this until you found it after the fact.
4. **Decide on Priya's status independently of the fix.** Whether you keep working with her is a separate question from remediation — and either way, the four videos need fixing regardless of what you decide about future work with her. Don't let firing her substitute for fixing the videos, and don't let fixing the videos substitute for deciding whether your vetting/contracting let this happen four times before anyone noticed.

**Bottom line: fix all four videos with real, conspicuous disclosure (on-screen/paid-promotion tag, not just description + pinned comment) immediately regardless of what you decide about Priya, and get legal counsel involved given the repeat pattern and the currently-trending video.**
