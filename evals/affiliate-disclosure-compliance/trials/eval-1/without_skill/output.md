Here are the two checks worth your 20 minutes, and why the rest can wait.

## Check 1: Fix the stream disclosure — wording + repetition

"Thanks to Thermobrew for making this happen" is a thank-you, not a disclosure. It never says the words "ad," "sponsored," or "paid partnership," so it doesn't clear the FTC's "clear and unambiguous" bar, and it's a one-shot at minute zero on a 90-minute stream where viewers join throughout — FTC's own livestreaming guidance calls that out by name as insufficient because latecomers never see it.

**Fix, costs 5 minutes:**

- Give Dana one line to say instead: _"Quick note — this stream is sponsored by Thermobrew, I'm paid to be here."_
- Have her repeat it roughly every 20–25 minutes (so ~4 times over 90 minutes), not just at the top.
- Keep the platform paid-partnership label on — it stays as a second layer, not the only layer.

This single change covers both jurisdictions at once: FTC wants clear language + recurrence on long-form live content, UK ASA/CAP wants unambiguous "ad" language up front — a repeated, explicit verbal disclosure satisfies both without needing two different scripts.

## Check 2: Make "ad" visible on-screen in each Story, not just in the caption

Relying on the caption is the weak link. Instagram Stories captions can sit off-screen, get covered by the UI, or go unread if someone taps through fast — UK ASA guidance explicitly says the platform label and captions alone aren't enough; "Ad" needs to be visible and prominent on the actual image/video content itself, upfront.

**Fix, costs 10 minutes total (~90 sec per Story):** Ask Dana to add a visible on-screen sticker/text overlay reading "AD" or "Paid partnership" to each of the 6 Stories, in addition to the caption and the platform label — not instead of them.

## Why these two and not others

Between them, these cover: the two platforms (Twitch + Instagram), the two audiences (US/FTC and UK/ASA — same fix satisfies both), and the two failure modes that actually get flagged in enforcement (vague language, and disclosure a viewer can plausibly miss). That's the coverage you're buying.

## Skip, given the time budget

- Auditing exact color/contrast/placement of the native platform labels — you already turned them on; diminishing returns on perfecting them.
- Separate US-specific vs UK-specific disclosure scripts — one strong, explicit disclosure clears both regimes, no need to draft two.
- Disclosing the 8% commission structure separately from the flat fee — under both FTC and ASA rules, one "material connection" disclosure covers any form of compensation; you don't need to itemize fee vs. commission on-screen.
- Screenshot/archive record-keeping of the disclosures — worth doing eventually, not worth spending Dana's or your 20 minutes on before Thursday.
