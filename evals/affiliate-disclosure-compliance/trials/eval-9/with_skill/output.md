### Your legal contact's two claims — both don't hold up

**1. "DSA Article 26 is what obliges her to label it, and she's used the toggle, so we're covered."**

Wrong statute, and wrong conclusion even if it were the right one.

- DSA Article 26(1) obliges the _platform_ (Meta) to identify ads in real time — it's not a duty on the creator at all.
- Article 26(2) just makes the platform give creators a _declare commercial content_ button and show _other users_ a marking. It supplies the toggle, not the obligation.
- Giulia's actual duty to disclose comes from **Italian national law** — the Codice del Consumo transposition of the EU UCPD (Directive 2005/29/EC), specifically Annex I point 11 (undisclosed advertorial content is blacklisted outright, no case-by-case test) and Article 7(2) (hidden commercial intent as a misleading omission) — layered with the **IAP Digital Chart**, Italy's operative influencer-disclosure standard.
- Practical rule from the reference set: "an unused platform declaration is a red flag; a used one never completes the check on its own." Toggling on branded content is necessary hygiene, not a substitute for a proper in-content disclosure. `[COUNSEL: confirm current AGCM enforcement posture on branded-content-tool-only cases — this review's UCPD/IAP citations are stable law but re-verify before relying on them for a live dispute]`

So the toggle doesn't discharge the disclosure duty on either asset, and neither asset actually has an adequate disclosure once you check placement (see tables below).

**2. "She's under 15k followers, so she's not a trader and consumer law doesn't reach her personally."**

There is no follower-count threshold in EU law. None. Whether an individual influencer counts as a "trader" under the UCPD is fact-specific — organisation, remuneration, scale — and the Commission's own 2021 UCPD Guidance treats this as requiring case-by-case assessment, not a bright line at 15k, 50k, or any other number. `[COUNSEL: Giulia's personal trader status stays genuinely open — don't assert either way without counsel]`

But this argument is also beside the point for your exposure: **Altavia Cosmetics is unambiguously a trader.** The same 2021 UCPD Guidance attributes advertorial breaches to "the influencer, the engaging brand, or both" — the brand's involvement isn't a fallback theory, it's a direct one. Whether Giulia personally qualifies as a trader has no bearing on whether Altavia is liable for paying for and publishing undisclosed (or inadequately disclosed) commercial content. "We're covered" doesn't follow even on your contact's own premise.

### Remediation

Both assets are non-compliant and (per the facts given) already live.

- **Feed post → edit in place.** The failing layer is caption placement, which is content Instagram lets her edit post-publish. Rewrite the caption per the fix above; no re-upload needed.
- **Reel → re-publish a corrected version, remove the original.** The missing element is baked into the video itself (on-screen overlay), and Instagram doesn't let you retroactively add an overlay to a live video. A caption/description edit cannot cure this — only a re-cut with the overlay added at the opening, re-published, with the original taken down.
- **Order**: action whichever asset is still actively earning impressions first — check current reach/analytics rather than assume. If both are still live and serving, do the Reel first: it needs the longer lead time (re-cut + re-upload) and is the one still accruing undisclosed exposure the longest once you account for prep time.
- Keep both `[COUNSEL]` markers open (Giulia's personal trader status; AGCM's current enforcement posture) until counsel clears them — don't let "she's under 15k" get repeated internally as settled.

**Overall: both assets FAIL. The toggle and the follower-count argument don't get you to "covered" on either one — you have two live, independently fixable placement defects, and the brand's own exposure doesn't depend on Giulia's personal trader status.**
