## Is 19% actually bad?

No — and the "30% industry standard" citation is almost certainly not a real benchmark, it's marketing copy. Three reasons to distrust it:

1. **Definition mismatch.** "Activation" has no standard meaning across affiliate networks — some count first click, some first referred sale, some first payout. A blog number without a stated definition is not comparable to anything.
2. **Window mismatch.** 30 days is an aggressive window for content-driven affiliates (fashion/B2C especially). Affiliates need to get approved into a creator's content calendar, shoot/post, and ride a purchase cycle — a lot of real activation lands in month 2–3, not month 1. Any "industry standard" quoted without specifying the window is meaningless.
3. **Vertical mismatch.** B2C fashion accessories affiliate pools are usually dominated by long-tail creators, coupon sites, and speculative sign-ups with low intent — very different economics from B2B SaaS or finance affiliate programs, which is where a lot of these "30%" blog numbers originate.

19% at 30 days for a B2C fashion program is in a normal-to-decent range. It is not evidence of a broken recruiting funnel, and it's not a reason to pause the program.

## What to tell your head of growth

- Ask her to produce the blog's methodology: definition of "activated," time window, vertical mix, sample size. If she can't, the number isn't a benchmark, it's an anecdote — don't let a program decision hinge on it.
- Before concluding anything from one cohort, pull the last 4–6 months of 30-day activation by cohort. If August (19%) sits within the normal range of June/July/etc., there's no signal here at all, just noise.
- Reframe the real question: is low activation a **recruitment-quality** problem (wrong affiliates) or an **onboarding/enablement** problem (right affiliates, slow ramp)? Check:
  - Activation curve by day 30 / 60 / 90 — if a meaningful chunk activates between day 30–90, that's an enablement/timing issue, not a recruiting one.
  - Activation rate segmented by acquisition source (manual outreach vs marketplace opt-in vs influencer partnerships) — this tells you whether specific channels are bringing in dead weight.
  - Whether affiliates who received a creative kit / product samples / onboarding call activate at meaningfully higher rates than those who didn't.
- Pausing the whole program to "re-recruit better affiliates" throws away signal you don't have yet. Fix the diagnosis before changing the strategy.

## What number to hold yourselves to going forward

Don't adopt an unsourced external number. Instead:

- Use your own trailing cohorts as the baseline (median of last 6 months), and track whether each new cohort moves up or down relative to that — not relative to a blog.
- Report 30-day activation as a **leading indicator**, but track 60/90-day activation as the real health metric for a content/fashion affiliate model, since that's where a realistic ramp lands.
- Set an improvement target tied to specific onboarding changes (e.g. "move 30-day activation from ~19% to mid-20s by adding a creative kit + Day-3 nudge email"), not a fixed universal target pulled from someone else's program.

## Which number do you report: 19% or 11%?

Report **19% (the August cohort)** as the primary KPI. The two numbers measure fundamentally different things, and reporting the wrong one misleads in either direction:

- **19% (30-day cohort)** is a _flow_ metric — it tells you whether your current recruiting + onboarding process is working right now. This is the number that reflects program health going forward.
- **11% (all-time across every affiliate ever approved)** is a _stock_ metric polluted by legacy weight — every affiliate you've ever approved, including years-old accounts recruited before current onboarding existed, one-off seasonal sign-ups, and marketplace opt-ins that never engaged, and none of them ever leave the denominator. This number will mechanically decay over time even if the current program is healthy, simply because you're accumulating never-active accounts faster than you're pruning them.

Present both to leadership, but label them differently:

- 19% = "is the current program working" (the actionable number)
- 11% = "we've never cleaned the affiliate roster" (a data-hygiene finding, not a performance verdict)

Separately, this points to a real fix worth doing regardless of the activation debate: **prune or archive affiliates who've never activated after a defined window** (e.g. 12 months with zero clicks/sales). That gives you a clean "active affiliate base" to report other metrics against (GMV per active affiliate, retention, etc.) instead of a denominator that only grows.
