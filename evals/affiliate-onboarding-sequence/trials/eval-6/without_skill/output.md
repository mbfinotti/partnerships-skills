# Partner onboarding for Reyvant — recommended design

**Don't copy the trial-signup drip.** That sequence is built for a completely different economics problem: hundreds/thousands of anonymous leads, near-zero cost per touch, and the goal is filtering + nudging at scale. Your partner motion is the opposite — 17 total relationships, ~$200k+ ACV each, a human with open calendar capacity, and CRO/consultancy buyers who need compliance depth (21 CFR Part 11, GxP validation, data residency, SOC 2) that no email drip can substitute for. At this volume, every partner should get a named point of contact and a real conversation, not a content funnel. Automation here should support the partner manager's calendar, not replace it.

---

## The sequence

**Phase 0 — Handoff (day 0, before onboarding starts)**
1. Sales briefs the partner manager: deal context, why this CRO signed, decision-makers, any commitments made.
2. Partner manager builds a 1-page onboarding plan per partner (not a template blast) — named stakeholders, target first-deal timeline, compliance docs they'll need.
3. Calendar invite sent directly for kickoff call — no "click here to schedule" email chain.

**Phase 1 — Kickoff call (week 1, live, human-run)**
4. 45–60 min call: goals, org chart on both sides, integration/compliance requirements, co-selling model (referral vs. reseller vs. embedded).
5. Immediately after: automated email fires — but it's a **recap of that specific call** (meeting notes + next steps), not generic copy. Trigger it off the calendar event, not off a fixed day-N schedule.

**Phase 2 — Enablement (weeks 2–4)**
6. Live-guided, not self-serve first pass: partner manager walks their team through the platform on a screen-share, tailored to the CRO's typical trial types.
7. *After* that live session, hand them the self-serve asset library as a reference — pitch deck, competitive battlecard, security/compliance packet, demo environment. Self-serve works once there's already a human relationship anchoring it; cold, it just becomes unread.
8. Certify at least one or two individuals on the partner side (product cert or "authorized to demo" status) — CROs need someone internally who can speak to Reyvant without looping you in every time.

**Phase 3 — First deal (weeks 4–12)**
9. Deal registration through the portal, but every registered deal triggers a human check-in from the partner manager within 48 hours — not an automated confirmation email only.
10. Joint call prep support for their first 1–2 prospect conversations (partner manager or SE joins).
11. Fast-track legal/security review — have your compliance packet pre-approved and ready, since this is where CRO deals stall.

**Phase 4 — Launch review (~day 90)**
12. Formal review: what worked, what's blocking their first close, adjust the plan. This is a real meeting, calendared like the kickoff.

**Phase 5 — Steady state**
13. Quarterly business review per partner, forever. At 20 partners this is ~5 QBRs/month — well within one partner manager's capacity.
14. Automated touches now make sense: product release notes, new asset library additions, renewal/cert expiry reminders. Low-stakes, informational, opt-out-able — the stuff drip automation is actually good for.

---

## Portal and asset library — repurpose, don't abandon

- **Keep the portal**, but its job is deal registration, doc repository, and cert tracking — not a drip delivery mechanism.
- **Keep the asset library**, but gate its introduction behind the live enablement session (step 7). Sequence-first, self-serve-second.
- Do **not** build lead-scoring or engagement-triggered nurture logic for this — you have 17–23 partners, you can just look at whether someone showed up to their QBR.

## What automation is actually for here

| Good fit for automation | Needs the partner manager |
|---|---|
| Meeting reminders, calendar logistics | Kickoff, enablement walkthrough, launch review, QBRs |
| Release notes, doc library updates | Deal registration follow-up |
| Cert/renewal expiry nudges | Anything touching compliance/legal blockers |
| Recap emails triggered by real calls | First-deal support |

## Metrics to actually track

- Time from signed → first live enablement session (target: <2 weeks)
- Time from signed → first registered deal (target: <90 days)
- % of partners with ≥1 certified individual
- QBR attendance rate

Bottom line: build a **playbook with light automation scaffolding around it**, not an automated sequence with human check-ins bolted on. At $200k+ per partner and low volume, the partner manager's time is the highest-leverage resource you have — design the sequence to protect and direct that time, not to route around it.
