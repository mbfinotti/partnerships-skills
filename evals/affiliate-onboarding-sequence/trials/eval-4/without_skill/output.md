# Sanity Check: Partner Referral Program

## What's solid
- 18 approvals in month one with a mix of boutique consultancies + 2 SIs is a reasonable start.
- Having a written drip sequence before scaling outreach is right — most programs skip this.
- Picking *an* activation metric at all puts you ahead of most launches, which measure nothing until quarter-end.

## The core problem: your activation metric hides the real risk

"First referral within 30 days" measures *volume of motion*, not *quality of motion*, and it will look fine right up until deals die in your 5-month pipeline. With an ACV of $60k, a bad referral costs you nothing to log but a lot of partner-manager time to qualify and kill. Build a ladder, not a single gate:

| Stage | Window | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Approved | Day 0 | — |
| Enabled | Day 0–14 | Completed drip / onboarding call attended |
| Activated | Day 0–30 | ≥1 referral submitted **that meets your ICP bar** |
| Qualified | Day 0–60 | Referral becomes a real opportunity (BANT/MEDDIC touched) |
| Revenue-proven | Day 0–150 (~5mo cycle) | Closed-won |

Report activation, but *manage* to qualified-rate. A partner who refers fast but 0% of it survives discovery is worse than one who takes 25 days to send one good deal.

## Segment the drip and the metric by partner type — this is your biggest gap

Boutique consultancies and SIs are not the same motion, and lumping them under one 14-day drip and one 30-day clock will misjudge both:

- **Boutique consultancies**: fast-moving, close to the client relationship, often *are* the champion. 30 days is realistic, maybe generous.
- **Systems integrators**: internal alignment, practice-lead buy-in, procurement/legal review before they'll attach your name to a client conversation. 30 days will likely read as a false failure for both of your SIs. Give them a 60–90 day activation window and a different "activated" definition — e.g., a scoped joint workshop counts, not just a lead handoff.

Don't let 2 SIs tank your blended activation rate against a 30-day bar built for boutiques.

## Capacity math you haven't done

One partner manager doing calls against 18 approvals/month is fine *this month*. It breaks the moment the program works, because success (more partners, more referrals) increases their load quadratically (onboarding calls + deal-support calls + re-engagement calls). Before month two:
- Decide whether onboarding is 1:1 or cohort (a biweekly group onboarding call scales far better than 18 separate calls, and boutiques especially don't need individual hand-holding).
- Reserve 1:1 time only for SIs and any partner showing high potential (existing footprint in target accounts).
- Define a hard ceiling (e.g., "1:1 calls only for partners requesting them or SIs") before you hit 40+ approvals and the manager becomes the bottleneck silently.

## Missing pieces to fill before you trust any number

1. **A hard definition of "referral."** A warm intro email, a form fill, and a scoped opportunity are three different things — if partners and your manager don't agree which one counts, the 30-day metric is unmeasurable, not just imprecise.
2. **A submission mechanism tied to CRM**, not verbal/Slack intros. Without this, "30 days" is whatever your partner manager remembers to log.
3. **ICP guidance for partners.** Without it, "activation" will reward partners who spray any warm body at you, especially boutiques trying to hit an early milestone.
4. **Deal registration / conflict rules.** With SIs and consultancies both close to accounts, you need a first-to-register or first-to-notify rule now, before the first double-claimed deal creates a partner-trust problem instead of a policy debate.
5. **Post-drip nurture for non-activators.** The 14-day drip ends; if no referral by day 20ish, is there a trigger for the partner manager to check in? Right now a partner who goes quiet just stays quiet until your 30-day report flags them as churned.
6. **Incentive/payout timing vs. the 5-month cycle.** Not mentioned — confirm partners know *when* they get paid (on qualified opp? on close?), because a 5-month gap with no interim acknowledgment (e.g., a qualified-stage bonus or at least a status update) will quietly kill re-referral behavior from your best partners.
7. **Sample-size discipline.** 18 is a small cohort — a single SI referring or not referring will swing your monthly activation rate by ~5.5 points. Don't react to month-to-month noise; look at trailing 3-month cohorts once you have them.

## Concrete next steps
- Split the drip: keep the 14-day version for consultancies, add an SI-specific track with a compliance/co-sell step and a 60–90 day activation window.
- Add "qualified within 60 days" as your real leading indicator toward revenue — it's the earliest signal you'll get before the 5-month close.
- Write a one-page ICP + referral-quality bar and hand it out before day 0, not after bad referrals show up.
- Move onboarding to cohort calls now, before growth makes the partner manager the constraint.
- Instrument referral submission through your CRM/PRM before next month's cohort, so the metric is even real.
