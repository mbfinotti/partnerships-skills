## Torvel Referral Program Terms

**Last updated:** [DATE]

### 1. Eligibility

1.1 You may participate as a **Referrer** if you are an individual with an active, paid Torvel subscription in good standing (no past-due invoices, no active fraud/abuse flag).
1.2 Free-trial users, cancelled accounts, and Torvel employees, contractors, and their immediate family members are not eligible to earn referral credit.

### 2. How It Works

2.1 Referrer shares a unique referral link or code ("**Referral Code**") with a prospective customer ("**Referred Company**").
2.2 A referral becomes a **Qualified Referral** only when _all_ of the following are met:

- (a) The Referred Company signs up using the Referral Code and was **not** an existing, prior, or in-pipeline Torvel customer, trial user, or opportunity in the preceding 12 months;
- (b) The Referred Company is a **distinct business entity** from the Referrer — different company domain, different billing entity, and not under common ownership or control with the Referrer's company;
- (c) Signup uses a **verified business email address on a company domain**. Signups from free consumer email providers (Gmail, Outlook.com, Yahoo, iCloud, etc.), disposable/temporary email services, or newly-registered domains with no verifiable business presence do not qualify;
- (d) The Referred Company subscribes to a **paid Torvel plan** and successfully completes **two consecutive monthly billing cycles** (or one full cycle on annual plans) without a chargeback, refund, or cancellation;
- (e) Payment is made via a **payment method and billing name distinct** from any used on the Referrer's own account.

2.3 Referral credit is issued only once the conditions above are satisfied — typically 60–75 days after signup. This delay is intentional and is not negotiable; it exists to confirm the referral is a genuine, sustained customer relationship.

### 3. Reward

3.1 Once a referral qualifies, Torvel issues **$20 (USD) or £16 (GBP)** in non-cash account credit to the Referrer's Torvel account (currency matches the Referrer's billing region).
3.2 Credit is applied automatically to the Referrer's next invoice(s), is **non-transferable, non-refundable, and not redeemable for cash**, and expires 12 months after issuance if unused.
3.3 **Program caps:** a Referrer may earn credit for a maximum of **10 Qualified Referrals in any rolling 12-month period** (i.e., up to $200/£160). Torvel may manually review any account approaching or exceeding this cap before releasing further credit.
3.4 One credit per unique Referred Company. Multiple signups from the same company, domain, or beneficial owner count as a single referral regardless of how many individuals sign up.

### 4. Fraud, Abuse & Program Integrity

4.1 Torvel reserves the right to withhold, delay, reverse, or claw back credit, and to suspend or terminate a Referrer's or Referred Company's account, where it reasonably believes:

- the referral relationship was fabricated, self-dealing, or circular (e.g., referrer and referred account share an owner, device, IP range, payment instrument, or address);
- the Referred Company was created using a disposable, alias, or non-business email, or was created solely to trigger the reward and shows no genuine product usage;
- signups are being generated at a rate or pattern consistent with automation or scripting;
- either account has a history of chargebacks, disputed payments, or Terms of Service violations.

4.2 Torvel may request reasonable verification (e.g., proof of business identity) before releasing credit on any referral it flags for review.
4.3 Abuse of the program may result in forfeiture of all pending and previously issued credit and termination of the Torvel account(s) involved, at Torvel's discretion.

### 5. Communications & Consent

5.1 Referrers are responsible for ensuring that any outreach they personally send to a prospective Referred Company complies with applicable law (e.g., CAN-SPAM in the US, PECR/UK GDPR in the UK) and that they have a lawful basis to contact the recipient. Torvel's own referral emails/links, where sent by Torvel on the Referrer's behalf, will carry an unsubscribe mechanism and comply with those same laws.
5.2 Referrers may not misrepresent Torvel, make claims about the product Torvel has not authorized, or spam unsolicited contacts at scale.

### 6. Tax

Referral credit may constitute taxable income or a taxable benefit depending on the Referrer's jurisdiction and tax status. Torvel does not provide tax advice; Referrers are responsible for their own tax reporting obligations.

### 7. Program Changes & Termination

Torvel may modify, suspend, or terminate this program, these Terms, or an individual's participation at any time, with reasonable notice for materially adverse changes to accrued-but-unpaid credit. Credit already issued and posted to an account prior to termination will be honored per Section 3.2.

### 8. Governing Law

8.1 For Referrers billed in USD, these Terms are governed by the laws of [Torvel's US state of incorporation], without regard to conflict-of-laws principles.
8.2 For Referrers billed in GBP, these Terms are governed by the laws of England and Wales, and nothing in these Terms limits a UK consumer's statutory rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 or other mandatory UK consumer protection law.

**Why the mechanics are shaped this way (flag for whoever reviews this):**

- The 60–75 day hold + two-billing-cycle requirement is the single strongest anti-gaming lever — it kills "sign up with a burner email, collect credit, cancel" farming outright, since the reward doesn't exist until the account has actually paid twice.
- Blocking free/consumer email domains at signup is a B2B-specific lever most B2C referral programs can't use — lean on it hard, since Torvel is B2B.
- The per-Referrer cap (10/12mo) limits worst-case exposure even if a fraud ring gets past the other checks; set the number based on your actual expected legitimate referral volume so it doesn't feel punitive to real power users.
- Dedup by company domain/billing entity (not just email address) stops the "10 people at the same company each sign up individually" loophole.

# 2. TikTok #ad-behind-the-fold — Compliance Verdict

**Short answer: not compliant, in both the US and the UK.** Get it fixed before it runs longer, and treat this as a signal to tighten the affiliate agreement, not a one-off.

## Why it fails — US (FTC)

- The FTC's Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) require a material-connection disclosure to be **"clear and conspicuous"** — unavoidable, and understandable without extra effort by the viewer.
- The FTC's own guidance ("Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers") calls out this exact pattern: a disclosure that only appears after the viewer taps **"more"** does not count, because most viewers never click through. Burying `#ad` there is treated the same as not disclosing at all.
- Liability isn't limited to the affiliate. The FTC has repeatedly said **brands are responsible for having a system to monitor and enforce disclosure compliance among their affiliates/influencers** — not just for having a policy on paper. As the paying brand, Torvel carries real exposure here even though the affiliate wrote the caption.

## Why it fails — UK (ASA / CMA)

- The CAP Code requires marketing communications to be **"obviously identifiable as such"** (rule 2.1/2.3) — the ASA and CMA's joint influencer guidance specifically says `#ad` must be **upfront and prominent**, i.e. visible before any "see more" cutoff, not buried in a hashtag block or below the fold.
- ASA rulings have upheld complaints on this exact fact pattern (disclosure technically present but hidden by truncation) — "technically disclosed" isn't a defense the ASA accepts.
- Since April 2025, the CMA has **direct fining powers under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024** for consumer-protection breaches (including hidden advertising) — this is no longer purely a self-regulatory (ASA) risk; it's now a real enforcement risk with financial penalties, and it can reach the brand, not only the creator.

## What "compliant" looks like instead

1. **Move `#ad` (or "Advert"/"Paid partnership with Torvel") to the very first line of the caption**, before any truncation point — not just present somewhere in the text.
2. **Use TikTok's native "Paid partnership" label** in addition to the caption tag. It's platform-enforced, appears regardless of caption length, and both FTC and ASA/CMA guidance point to platform tools as best practice.
3. **Add an on-screen burned-in text disclosure and/or a verbal "this is a paid ad" in the first few seconds of the video itself.** Captions are the weakest disclosure channel — many viewers watch with sound off or never expand text — video-native disclosure is what actually gets seen.
4. Do this **retroactively on the live post** (ask the affiliate to edit the caption and add the platform label now) rather than only going forward.

## Action for Torvel specifically

- Contact the affiliate today and have them fix the existing post (caption edit + add the TikTok paid-partnership toggle).
- Update the affiliate agreement to make disclosure placement (not just presence) a contractual requirement, with a spot-check process — this is what regulators mean by "having a monitoring program," and it's your best defense if a _different_ affiliate slips up later.
- If this affiliate has other live posts, sweep them for the same issue before someone else (a competitor, a journalist, or the ASA/CMA directly) finds it first.
