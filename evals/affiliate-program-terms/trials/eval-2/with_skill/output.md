# Prohibited Tactics & Enforcement — Rewritten Section

**This is a draft for review by qualified counsel, not legal advice. Program terms are legally binding — do not publish until counsel clears every `[LEGAL REVIEW]` marker below.**

Your current line — *"Affiliates shall not engage in any unethical, inappropriate or abusive marketing practices"* — is a catch-all, and catch-alls prohibit nothing in practice: under the standard drafting principle for these agreements, any tactic not explicitly named is implicitly permitted. That's exactly why you couldn't act on the popunders or the fabricated code short of termination — neither was *named*, so neither triggered anything short of the most drastic remedy. The fix is to enumerate, and to give yourself response options between "ignore it" and "kick them out."

Below is a full replacement section: an enumerated ban list (including the two tactics that just cost you) and a graduated enforcement ladder. Two sub-positions are program choices, not legal questions — I've defaulted them to the tightest common setting given what you just caught, and flagged them so you can confirm or change without a rewrite.

## 6. Prohibited Tactics & Enforcement

### 6.1 Prohibited Tactics

Affiliate shall not, directly or through any third party, agent, sub-affiliate, or automated system, engage in any of the following:

1. **Cookie stuffing and forced clicks.** Setting or attempting to set a tracking cookie without a genuine, voluntary user click, including via 0x0 or hidden iframes, **popunders, popups, forced redirects**, or any browser window opened without direct user action. *This is the clause that covers the popunder traffic you found — it did not exist as a named ban before.*
2. **Unauthorized discount codes.** Publishing, advertising, or distributing any discount, promo, or coupon code that was not issued to that Affiliate by Company, including expired codes, internal/employee codes, or codes fabricated to resemble a genuine Company code. Any commission attributed to an unauthorized code is void regardless of order value. *This is the clause that covers the code you never issued — it did not exist as a named ban before.*
3. **Typosquatting.** Registering or using domains that are misspellings, permutations, or confusable variants of Company's trademarks or domains.
4. **Adware and browser-extension injection.** Software that injects, overwrites, or drops Affiliate's tracking cookie or link during a user's normal browsing session without a genuine, disclosed user action.
5. **Spam and unsolicited communications.** Affiliate links or codes in unsolicited email, SMS, or messaging-app blasts.
6. **Misleading claims and fake reviews.** Fabricated results, fake or AI-generated reviews, or undisclosed insider/incentivized reviews. `[LEGAL REVIEW: 16 CFR Part 465 (FTC Reviews Rule) exposure if any Affiliate traffic is US-facing]`
7. **Brand impersonation.** Presenting as "[Company] Official," "[Company] Support," "[Company] Sales," or otherwise implying an official or employment relationship, in handles, ads, or domains.
8. **Self-referral and incentivized traffic.** Earning commission on Affiliate's own purchases or those of friends/family; manufacturing referrals through undisclosed incentives; double-claiming commission on the same transaction across multiple programs.
9. **Unauthorized marketplace listings.** Listing Company's products for sale on Amazon, eBay, or any third-party marketplace without Company's prior written consent.
10. **AI-generated spam content.** Mass, low-quality, or auto-generated content produced primarily to place affiliate links rather than to inform.
11. **Trademark / brand-keyword bidding.** [DEFAULT — confirm or change] Affiliate is strictly prohibited from bidding on Company's trademarked terms (exact match, phrase, broad, misspellings, permutations, and character substitutions) in any paid-search campaign, from using Company's marks in paid-search ad copy, and must add Company's marks as negative keywords. `[LEGAL REVIEW: this ban is contractual only — search engines will not enforce it, and keyword bidding on a mark is generally lawful; it functions here as a termination trigger, not an injunctive one]`

    *Not triggered by either incident you described — flagged only because a bare list without it would leave the same enumerate-or-permit gap on a different tactic. If you have a partner whose brand-term traffic you want to keep, tell me and I'll swap this for a modifier-allowance or named-partner exception instead.*
12. **Browser extension use.** [DEFAULT — confirm or change] Browser extensions, toolbars, or plugins that attach an affiliate link, code, or cookie are banned outright.

    *Defaulted to a flat ban because nothing in what you described suggests an extension partner driving material revenue. If one does, this needs a stand-down obligation instead of a ban — different clause, tell me if that's the case.*

Any violation of this Section 6.1 is a **material breach** of this Agreement.

### 6.2 Enforcement Ladder

Company may respond to a violation of Section 6.1 with any of the following, applied in escalating order at Company's discretion based on severity:

1. **Written warning** with a cure period of [X] business days.
2. **Voiding of commissions** generated through the violating tactic, from the date the tactic began.
3. **Account suspension** pending investigation, during which no new commissions accrue.
4. **Termination for cause**, effective immediately, without the notice period applicable to for-convenience termination.
5. **Recovery of already-paid commissions** attributable to the violation, and reservation of Company's right to pursue damages.

For **fraud, cookie stuffing, or forced-click tactics** (Section 6.1(1)), Company may skip directly to termination for cause and recovery under items 4–5 without first issuing a warning or suspension.

Company will not use a permanent reduction of Affiliate's commission rate as a response to a Section 6.1 violation; a rate cut neither stops the tactic nor removes the affiliate, and is not an available remedy under this ladder.

## What I flagged and why

- **Coupon policy (item 2)** is drafted at the "restricted" setting — only issued codes, expired/internal codes void the commission — because that's the specific gap the fabricated-code incident exposed. If you actually want to ban coupon/deal sites entirely, or permit them without restriction, say so and I'll swap the one clause.
- **Brand-bidding (item 11)** and **browser extensions (item 12)** aren't things you flagged as problems, but a rewritten section can't leave them as unstated gaps — I defaulted both to the strictest, lowest-effort position (strict ban) since nothing in your message suggests a partner you'd want an exception for.
- **Two clauses this ladder depends on but that live elsewhere in the agreement, not this section:** automatic revocation of the brand-asset licence on termination, and what happens to an offending affiliate's *pending* (not-yet-paid) commissions. Your one-line clause almost certainly doesn't have either. They're the two most commonly missing clauses paired with this exact ladder — tell me if you want those drafted next; they're each one clause.
