I'm not a lawyer, and this doesn't replace review by counsel — especially before you enforce it against real affiliates. But here's the direct answer and the drafting.

## Is "effective Monday, no notice" fine?

**Not for your EU/UK affiliates. For the rest, probably, but I'd still notice everyone.**

The reason: an "we can change these terms any time without notice" clause doesn't override the **EU Platform-to-Business Regulation (2019/1150)** and its **UK equivalent** (retained post-Brexit as the Online Intermediation Services for Business Users (Enforcement) Regulations 2020). Both apply to providers of online intermediation services toward business users — an affiliate program (you route consumer transactions to affiliates' referred traffic in exchange for commission) fits that definition in most regulators' reading. The regulation requires:

- **Minimum 15 days' notice** before a T&C change takes effect for EU/UK business users.
- Exceptions only for (a) a legal/regulatory obligation forcing immediate action, or (b) an emergency need to protect the service from an existing threat (fraud, malware, security).
- The affiliate must be able to **terminate during the notice window** without penalty if they don't accept the change.
- A boilerplate "we may change at any time" clause does **not** itself qualify as an exception — it's the thing the regulation constrains, not a way around it.

Consequence for your ~300 EU/UK affiliates: rolling this out Monday with zero notice is a straight P2B violation, enforceable by national regulators and by affiliates directly (some EU member states allow private actions; UK enforcement runs through the CMA/sector regulators plus contract remedies). Fines and reputational cost aside, an affiliate you terminate under a non-compliant amendment has a real argument that the amendment never validly bound them.

Two clauses, two different urgency arguments:

- **Fake-review ban**: you likely *can* justify shortened/immediate notice for EU/UK under the "legal compliance" carve-out — the UK's Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 and the EU's Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (as amended by the Omnibus Directive) already make fake reviews illegal. You're not creating a new obligation, you're codifying one that already binds you and them.
- **Brand-bidding ban**: this is a commercial/IP policy choice, not a pre-existing legal mandate. It doesn't qualify for the shortcut. Full 15-day notice applies to EU/UK affiliates.

**Recommended sequencing**, rather than one Monday cutover:
1. Send the amendment notice to **all 1,100 affiliates today**, effective in 15 days for the brand-bidding ban (satisfies P2B for everyone, not just EU/UK — simpler than segmenting).
2. Make the fake-review clause effective **Monday for everyone** — defensible as codifying existing law.
3. Deal with the two live offenders **now**, separately from the amendment: check whether your current terms already have a general trademark-misuse, unauthorized-advertising, or deceptive-marketing clause. Most affiliate agreements do. If so, suspend those two affiliates under that existing clause today — you don't need the new clause to stop current harm, and you avoid the appearance of applying a rule retroactively to conduct that predates it.
4. Don't claw back or penalize brand-bidding activity that occurred **before** the new ban's effective date under the new clause itself — apply it prospectively. Retroactive penalty under a unilaterally-imposed new term is the weakest part of any enforcement action if challenged.

If you don't have an existing clause covering trademark misuse and need to stop the two affiliates before the 15 days run, that's an immediate, individual enforcement action (cease-and-desist + suspension pending investigation) under general "deceptive or unauthorized marketing" language most programs carry — not something to wait on the amendment for.

## Amendment / notice clause (update to your general "modification" section)

> **Amendments to these Terms.** Company may amend these Terms at any time. For any amendment that a Business User (as defined under Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 and the UK Online Intermediation Services for Business Users (Enforcement) Regulations 2020) is entitled to advance notice of, Company will provide at least fifteen (15) days' notice before the amendment takes effect for that Business User, published on the Program's terms page and sent to the affiliate's registered email address. An affiliate who does not accept an amendment may terminate their participation in the Program at no cost, by written notice to Company, at any time before the amendment's effective date. Where an amendment is required to comply with a legal or regulatory obligation, or to address an existing risk to the Program's security or its users, Company may make the amendment effective on shorter notice, or immediately, to the extent required by that obligation or risk. No amendment applies retroactively to conduct that occurred, or commissions that accrued, before its effective date, except as required by applicable law.

## Brand-bidding ban (new clause)

> **Prohibition on Trademark Bidding.** Affiliate shall not bid on, purchase, or use, directly or indirectly (including through a search engine, social media ad platform, or any other paid channel), any keyword, search term, ad copy, domain name, or campaign target that consists of, incorporates, or is confusingly similar to Company's trademarks, trade names, product names, or any misspelling or variant thereof, whether alone or in combination with other terms (including "coupon," "discount," "promo," "deal," or similar). This prohibition applies regardless of whether Affiliate's landing page ultimately links to Company's site. Company may, at its discretion, treat a violation of this clause as grounds for immediate suspension of Affiliate's account, forfeiture of commissions generated through the violating campaign, and termination of the Agreement, without prejudice to any other remedy available to Company.

*Effective date: apply prospectively only, per the amendment clause above.*

## Fake-review ban (new clause)

> **Prohibition on Fake or Incentivized Reviews.** Affiliate shall not create, submit, commission, purchase, or facilitate any review, rating, testimonial, or endorsement of Company or its products that (a) is not based on the reviewer's genuine experience, (b) is written or posted by someone with no actual experience of the product or service, (c) misrepresents the reviewer's identity or affiliation with Company or Affiliate, or (d) is exchanged for payment, free product, commission, or other incentive without clear and conspicuous disclosure of that incentive, as required by applicable consumer protection law (including the UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 and the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive as amended). Affiliate shall promptly remove any review found to violate this clause upon Company's request. A violation is grounds for immediate suspension or termination under this Agreement.
