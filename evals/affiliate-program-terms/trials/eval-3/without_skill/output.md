Here are the two clauses. Drop them into your Affiliate Program Terms (I've labeled them for a "Commissions and Payouts" or "Termination" section — adjust numbering to fit your existing document).

**Forfeiture Upon Termination**

If Halvard Tools terminates an affiliate's participation in the Program for any reason, all amounts associated with that affiliate's account — including pending commissions, approved but unpaid commissions, and any other accrued balance — are immediately and permanently forfeited. The affiliate has no right to payment of any such amounts, and Halvard Tools has no obligation to pay them, regardless of the reason for termination.

**Forfeiture for Inactivity**

If an affiliate's account records no clicks for 180 consecutive days, the affiliate is deemed inactive. Upon reaching 180 consecutive days without a click, the affiliate's entire balance — including pending and approved commissions — is automatically and permanently forfeited, and Halvard Tools has no obligation to pay any such amounts. Halvard Tools may, but is not required to, notify the affiliate before or after forfeiture occurs.

A couple of things worth flagging before you publish these, not to slow you down, just so you've made the call knowingly:

- **"Terminate for any reason" + forfeiture together** is the part most likely to draw pushback or scrutiny (FTC endorsement-guide attention on affiliate programs has increased, and a few states scrutinize forfeiture-on-termination clauses as unconscionable if the termination itself is discretionary/for convenience). If you ever terminate someone right before a big payout was due, "any reason" is what protects you — but it's also the phrase a plaintiff's lawyer would circle first.
- I didn't add a carve-out for termination due to *fraud/ToS violation* vs. termination *for convenience* — some programs split those (harsher forfeiture for cause, prorated payout for convenience-terminations) to reduce that exact risk. You said "doesn't matter," so I kept it uniform — just confirming that's intentional.
