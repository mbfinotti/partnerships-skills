### 14. Effect of Termination on Commission Balances

Upon termination of this Agreement by Halvard Tools for any reason, including but not limited to termination for convenience, termination for cause, or termination for breach of the Prohibited Tactics set out in Section [X], any and all commissions credited to the Affiliate's account as of the effective date of termination — whether in **Pending**, **Approved**, or any other status short of a completed payout — are automatically and permanently forfeited. The Affiliate waives any claim to payment of such amounts, and Halvard Tools has no further payment obligation with respect to them.

`[LEGAL REVIEW: Blanket forfeiture of all earned commissions regardless of termination reason is a legally contested position. It has unresolved tension with US state unclaimed-property/escheatment statutes — earned-but-unpaid commissions can become reportable to the state as unclaimed property after a 1–5 year dormancy period in many states, and a forfeiture clause does not necessarily extinguish that reporting obligation. Counsel should confirm whether this clause is enforceable as drafted, or whether it needs a carve-out (e.g., forfeiture on fraud/breach only, with non-fraud terminations paid out to the termination date) before publication.]`

_Narrower alternative counsel may prefer, per standard drafting guidance for this clause type:_

> _"Upon termination for convenience or program discontinuation, Halvard Tools will pay all commissions that were validly Approved as of the termination date, on the normal payment schedule, less any amounts subject to an open Clawback under Section [X]. Upon termination for cause — including fraud, breach of the Prohibited Tactics, or provision of false account information — Halvard Tools may forfeit commissions attributable to the conduct giving rise to termination; commissions unrelated to that conduct remain payable."_

### 15. Dormancy Forfeiture

If an Affiliate's tracking link generates zero clicks for 180 consecutive calendar days, the Affiliate's account is deemed dormant. Upon dormancy, any commission balance in the account — Pending or Approved — is forfeited, and the account may be closed at Halvard Tools' discretion.

`[LEGAL REVIEW: Same escheatment tension as above applies to dormancy-triggered forfeiture — several states treat this fact pattern (an unpaid balance sitting dormant for a defined period) as exactly the trigger for unclaimed-property reporting, not a private right to forfeit. This exact "180 consecutive days, full balance" structure exists in at least one large publisher program, but counsel should confirm it survives scrutiny under the Affiliate's state(s) of residence before you rely on the precedent.]`

To keep this enforceable in practice (not just on paper), the clause needs two components layered in even though you didn't ask for them — drafting it without them is the version most likely to draw a dispute or regulator attention:

- **Prior notice**: Halvard Tools will notify the Affiliate by email at least 30 days before dormancy forfeiture takes effect, giving the Affiliate an opportunity to reactivate.
- **Reactivation path**: An Affiliate who generates a click before the forfeiture date automatically resets the 180-day clock and avoids forfeiture.

`[LEGAL REVIEW: Notice and reactivation aren't optional extras — draft the clause without them and you're relying purely on the forfeiture right, which is the weaker position under the same escheatment analysis above.]`

**Two things to flag to Halvard Tools before this goes live:**

1. **US-only today, but note the exposure is state-by-state.** Escheatment/unclaimed-property rules vary by state and the dormancy period that triggers reporting (1–5 years) is longer than your 180-day clock — that gap doesn't make the clause safe, it just means the conflict shows up later, when a state audits unclaimed property rather than when an affiliate disputes a balance. Counsel should confirm this is acceptable as designed.
2. **This is a contract-termination and dispute-avoidance clause, not a guaranteed collection right** — an affiliate can still contest forfeiture, particularly on an Approved (not just Pending) balance, since "Approved" typically means the underlying transaction already cleared validation and is treated closer to earned wages/receivables in some disputes.

Both clauses are otherwise drafted exactly to your spec: no distinction between pending and approved, no fraud carve-out, forfeiture is automatic. Get counsel sign-off before publishing, given the `[LEGAL REVIEW]` markers above.
