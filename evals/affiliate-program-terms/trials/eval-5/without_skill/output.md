Here's the brand-bidding section, written to slot into Ember & Co's affiliate program terms as a standalone clause. It's built around a whitelist mechanism so no blanket bidding rights are granted to any affiliate — including Glosslight — and approval stays case-by-case, revocable, and time-limited (since no one is dedicated to policing it day-to-day).

---

## [X]. Brand Bidding and Trademark Use in Paid Search

### [X].1 Default rule: no brand bidding

Unless a keyword is listed on the **Brand Term Whitelist** (Exhibit A) and specifically approved in writing for that Affiliate, the Affiliate may not:

- Bid on, or allow automated/broad-match campaigns to trigger on, "Ember & Co," "Ember Co," or any spelling variant, misspelling, phonetic equivalent, or close variation of these terms, in any search engine, in any match type (exact, phrase, broad, or broad match modifier).
- Combine the Ember & Co brand name with any other word (e.g. "discount," "coupon," "promo code," "review," "sale") in a bid keyword.
- Use "Ember & Co," "Ember Co," or any Ember & Co trademark, logo, or product name in ad copy headlines, descriptions, display URLs, or destination URL paths.
- Register a domain, subdomain, or social handle containing the Ember & Co name or a confusingly similar variant.

This default applies to every Affiliate equally, regardless of size or tenure in the program.

### [X].2 Whitelist exception process

An Affiliate may request approval to bid on specific brand-adjacent terms by submitting, in writing, the **exact keyword string(s)** it wants to bid on — not a category or pattern. Example: `ember co discount` is a valid request; "brand + discount terms" is not.

Each request is reviewed and decided individually. Ember & Co may approve, deny, or approve with conditions (e.g. specific match type only, specific ad copy required, geo-restricted). Approval:

- Is affiliate-specific and non-transferable. It does not extend to other affiliates, other keyword variants, or the naked brand term.
- Does not create precedent. Approving a term for one affiliate does not obligate Ember & Co to approve the same or a similar term for another.
- Is granted or denied at Ember & Co's sole discretion. Ember & Co is not required to give a reason for denial.

Approved terms are recorded in Exhibit A with: the exact keyword string, the affiliate it applies to, the approval date, and the expiration date (see [X].5). No keyword is live until it appears in Exhibit A with an active (non-expired) status.

### [X].3 What gets weighed in a review

Ember & Co considers, without being limited to:

- Whether the term is a long-tail, modifier-qualified term (e.g. brand + "discount") rather than the naked brand term or a misspelling designed to intercept branded search traffic generally.
- Whether the affiliate's landing page adds genuine value (reviews, comparisons, verified codes) rather than redirecting straight to Ember & Co's own site.
- The affiliate's compliance history in the program.
- Estimated incrementality vs. cannibalization of Ember & Co's own branded search spend.

### [X].4 Conditions on approved bidding

Where a term is approved, the Affiliate must still:

- Disclose the affiliate relationship clearly on the ad and landing page (FTC-compliant disclosure).
- Not use Ember & Co's trademark or logo in the ad headline, display URL, or destination domain.
- Not imply official/authorized status, or a discount that does not exist.
- Stop bidding immediately upon written notice from Ember & Co, with no cure period required.

### [X].5 Expiration, review, and revocation

- Every whitelist approval expires **90 days** after the grant date unless explicitly renewed in writing. There is no auto-renewal. An expired entry reverts to the default prohibition in [X].1.
- Ember & Co may revoke any approval at any time, for any reason or no reason, effective immediately on notice.
- Ember & Co does not commit to a fixed audit schedule or turnaround time for requests; reviews happen on a rolling, best-effort basis. Affiliates bidding on an unapproved or expired term are responsible for the resulting violation regardless of whether Ember & Co has actively monitored for it.

### [X].6 Consequences of unauthorized brand bidding

Bidding on a non-whitelisted or expired term is a material breach. Ember & Co may, at its discretion: withhold or claw back commission on affected orders, suspend the affiliate, or terminate the agreement, in addition to any other remedy available at law (including trademark infringement).

A few notes outside the clause itself, since you'll likely be the one running this:

- I deliberately didn't pre-approve `ember co discount` or name Glosslight anywhere in the Terms — putting a specific affiliate's name in the legal text creates a precedent argument ("you did it for them, why not us") and locks you into language the moment any other affiliate reads the Terms. Approve Glosslight's specific strings through the Exhibit A process instead, after the Terms are signed.
- The 90-day expiration exists because you said no one owns ongoing enforcement — it defaults back to "no bidding" on its own instead of relying on someone remembering to revoke it.
- If Glosslight balks at case-by-case (they might, since it's slower than a blanket yes), the fallback pressure point is that leverage cuts both ways: if they're your biggest affiliate, losing them hurts, but a blanket brand-bid grant to your top affiliate also means you can't easily deny the same to #2 through #10 without a clean paper trail for why they're different — which this clause gives you.
