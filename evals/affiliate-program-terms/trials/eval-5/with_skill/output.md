## 6. Prohibited Tactics & Enforcement — Brand-Keyword Bidding

**6.1 General prohibition.** Except as expressly permitted under the Approved Brand-Term List (§6.3), Affiliate shall not bid on, purchase, or otherwise activate paid search, paid social, or other paid placements triggered by:

(a) "Ember & Co," "Ember and Co," "Ember&Co," or any of the Marks identified in Schedule A, in any match type (broad, phrase, or exact);
(b) any misspelling, phonetic variant, character substitution, or permutation of the foregoing;
(c) any of the foregoing combined with a modifier term (including but not limited to "discount," "coupon," "promo," "sale," "review," "code," or "deal"), whether or not that combination appears on the Approved Brand-Term List for a different Affiliate.

Affiliate shall add each Mark, and its known misspellings and permutations, as a negative keyword across all paid campaigns not expressly authorized under §6.3. Affiliate shall not use any Mark in ad copy, display URLs, or landing-page URLs for paid placements, and shall not direct-link paid-search traffic to Company's domain in a manner implying Company sponsorship of the ad itself.

_`[LEGAL REVIEW: this prohibition is contractual only — search engines will not enforce a private bidding restriction, and bidding on a brand term as a keyword is generally lawful in most jurisdictions. Only Mark use in ad copy is independently actionable as trademark infringement. Treat this clause as a termination trigger, not an injunctive remedy.]`_

**6.2 Effect of unauthorized bidding.** Any bid, impression, click, or conversion generated in violation of §6.1 is a material breach and triggers the enforcement ladder at §6.5, up to and including immediate termination for cause and clawback of resulting commissions under §15, regardless of whether the underlying sale would otherwise have qualified.

**6.3 Approved Brand-Term List.** Company may, in its sole discretion exercised in accordance with this section, authorize a named Affiliate to bid on specific brand-inclusive search terms ("Approved Terms"). Approved Terms are maintained in a schedule incorporated by reference (the "Approved Brand-Term List"), not in the body of these Terms, so that additions, narrowings, or removals do not themselves require an amendment under §17.

(a) **Request and criteria.** An Affiliate may request inclusion of a specific term or term pattern. Company will evaluate requests against: (i) the Affiliate's trailing 12-month tracked revenue share; (ii) whether the Affiliate's content on the requested term is substantive and not a bare redirect or coupon-scrape page; (iii) absence of any unresolved prohibited-tactics violation in the preceding 12 months; and (iv) Company's assessment of incremental versus cannibalized paid-search spend. Company will state the specific ground(s) for any refusal in writing.

_`[LEGAL REVIEW: stating grounds for refusal is drafted here because differential treatment of business-user affiliates on a platform-type program can trigger EU/UK Platform-to-Business (P2B) Regulation obligations — a stated-reasons requirement and, for restriction/suspension/termination decisions, prescribed notice periods. Confirm with counsel whether this program is in scope before relying on this clause as sufficient P2B compliance if any approved or requesting Affiliate is an EU/UK business established there.]`_

(b) **Scope of approval.** Approval is specific to the exact term(s) listed, the match type(s) listed, and the Affiliate named — it does not extend to other brand-inclusive terms, other match types, or any other Affiliate, and does not waive §6.1 for anything not listed.

(c) **Standing review obligation.** Company will re-review the Approved Brand-Term List no less than quarterly, confirming (i) each listed Affiliate's live ads still match the approved term and match type exactly, and (ii) no non-listed Affiliate has begun bidding on a listed term. **`[LEGAL REVIEW / OPERATIONAL: name the accountable owner and cadence here before publication — an unreviewed list is not distinguishable, in practice or in a P2B dispute, from an unwritten open-bidding policy.]`**

(d) **Revocation.** Company may remove any term or Affiliate from the Approved Brand-Term List at any time on written notice, effective immediately for cause (a §6.1/6.2 violation, brand harm, or a change in ad content) or on 15 days' notice otherwise. Removal is not a termination of the affiliate relationship and does not itself trigger clawback absent an independent violation.

**6.4 Non-precedential effect.** Approval of one Affiliate for one term creates no entitlement for any other Affiliate, and no course of dealing or prior approval binds Company's discretion as to any future request.

**6.5 Enforcement ladder (brand-bidding violations).** A violation of §6.1 not otherwise addressed by an existing Approved Brand-Term List entry follows the general enforcement ladder at §[enforcement ladder section]: (1) written warning with a same-day cure requirement given the immediacy of ad spend harm; (2) voiding of all commissions from the violating campaign; (3) immediate suspension pending investigation; (4) termination for cause; (5) recovery of already-paid commissions attributable to the violation. Company may skip directly to (3) or (4) where the violation involves Mark use in ad copy or a misspelling/permutation clearly designed to evade detection.
