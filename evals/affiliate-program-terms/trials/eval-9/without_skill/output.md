Before the clause — a quick factual correction, because it changes what "light" safely means here:

The FTC Endorsement Guides themselves are non-binding guidance, but non-disclosure of a paid/affiliate relationship is enforced as a **deceptive act under Section 5 of the FTC Act**, which does carry penalties (consent orders, redress, and civil penalties up to ~$51,744/violation for knowing violations). The FTC also finalized a **binding rule** in 2024 (16 CFR Part 465, effective Oct 2024) covering fake/deceptive reviews and undisclosed insider reviews — this is a real rule with real fines, not guidance. And supplements specifically are one of the FTC's most-litigated advertising categories.

On the UK side, undisclosed paid endorsement is a banned practice under the **Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008** (Schedule 1) — enforceable criminally (unlimited fine / up to 2 years) or via CMA civil enforcement, and CMA fines now reach up to 10% of global turnover under the 2024 Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act. With 60 affiliates publishing written reviews, that's exactly the fact pattern regulators target as disguised advertising.

So a single "comply with applicable law" line leaves the disclosure obligation undefined and unenforceable against affiliates who skip it — which is the actual exposure. Below is still a short, non-bureaucratic clause, just one that specifies what compliance means so it's usable in practice.

---

**5. Disclosure Obligations**

**5.1** Affiliate must clearly and conspicuously disclose their financial relationship with Chandlerwick Labs in any content promoting the Products — including written reviews, blog posts, social media, video, and email.

**5.2** Disclosure must appear before the affiliate link or product claim (not only in a footer, bio, or hyperlinked disclaimer) and use plain language a reasonable reader would understand (e.g., "This post contains affiliate links — I earn a commission on purchases," or "#ad"/"#affiliate" on social platforms).

**5.3** Written reviews must state that the piece is a paid/affiliate review. If Affiliate received free product or other consideration beyond commission, that must be disclosed separately from the affiliate-link disclosure.

**5.4** Affiliate is responsible for complying with disclosure laws in their own jurisdiction, including (as applicable) the FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) and FTC Trade Regulation Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465) for US audiences, and the CAP Code and Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 for UK audiences.

**5.5** Chandlerwick Labs may request evidence of disclosure (e.g., a screenshot or link) and may withhold commission or terminate this Agreement for any content found non-compliant.

---

This isn't legal advice — have UK and US counsel confirm wording before publishing, particularly clause 5.4's jurisdiction references, since advertising law citations date quickly.
