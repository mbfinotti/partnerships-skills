No — running the full 12-touch, 3-channel rotation on all 180 sites doesn't fit your time budget or the actual response curve. Here's the math and what to do instead.

**Why not as designed**

- 180 prospects × 12 touches = 2,160 touches over ~13 weeks ≈ 166/week ≈ 1.4 min/touch at 4h/week. That only works if every touch is a one-click template — LinkedIn DMs and contact forms aren't.
- LinkedIn caps cold connection requests around 100–200/week; rotating DMs into 180 prospects across several rounds burns that fast and risks a temporary restriction, killing the whole channel.
- Contact forms repeated on the same site multiple times reads as spam to whoever triages it — the opposite of what you want from a future partner.
- Most replies in cold B2B outreach land in the first 4–6 touches; touches 7–12 add little beyond forcing warm prospects to eventually answer, at a cost that isn't proportional here.

**What to run instead**

1. Quick tier pass on the 180 (traffic/DR, topical fit) — 30 min, one-time.
2. Default sequence for everyone, ~3–4 weeks, not 3 months:
   - Day 0: email 1
   - Day 4: email 2 (different angle)
   - Day 7: LinkedIn connect + short note
   - Day 14: email 3 (breakup/last call)
   - Contact form: one-time fallback only if no email/LinkedIn found — never a repeated channel.
3. Automate the email leg (sequencer tool) so it costs ~0 of your weekly hours. Spend your 4h/week on LinkedIn touches and replying to anyone who engages.
4. Reserve the long 8–12 touch, multi-month treatment for a small top tier (~20–30 highest-value sites) or anyone who shows a signal (opened, clicked, viewed profile) — extend those specifically, not the whole list.

Net: you keep the program moving inside 4h/week, protect your LinkedIn account and domain reputation, and still give the prospects worth chasing the full 12-touch persistence — just not everyone.
