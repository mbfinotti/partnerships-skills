## Short answer: No — run 5 touches over ~4 weeks, not the 12-attempt cadence

For a solo operator at 4 hours/week, the 12-attempt cadence is the wrong call for this list. Here's why, and what to run instead.

### ❌ Why not the 12-attempt cadence

The "twelve attempts, three channels, three months" advice you read is real, but it's generic cold-outreach advice, not affiliate-specific — and it optimizes for reach, not for your actual constraint.

Ranked against each other, the two cadences split like this:

| Axis | Winner |
|---|---|
| Efficiency (replies per hour you spend) | **5 touches** |
| Value (% of the list eventually reached) | 12 attempts |
| Effort required | 12 attempts costs more |
| Compliance exposure | 12 attempts costs more |

You have one scarce resource: hours, not prospects. 180 is plenty of list. Efficiency is the only axis that matters when hours are the ceiling, and 5 touches wins it.

The 12-attempt cadence isn't just "longer" — it changes in *kind*. The 5-touch sequence front-loads the work: touch 1 is personalized, touches 2-5 are templated per tier, so a batch runs in a few hours a week. The 12-attempt version multiplies total sends by roughly 2.5x, and every attempt that switches channel also costs a lookup (find the LinkedIn profile, find the contact form) before you can even send. That turns a campaign into a standing job — which is exactly what you don't have capacity for.

⚠️ Rotating in a contact form and a LinkedIn DM per prospect also stretches your compliance surface for no extra revenue signal: three months of persistence per prospect instead of four weeks, and LinkedIn DMs must stay manual and low-volume regardless — no automation, whatever tool promises "safe" volumes.

### ✅ What to run instead

**Default: 5 touches over ~4 weeks, for the whole 180.**

| Touch | Day | Channel | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Email | Fit + plain economics (your commission/cookie/payout, exactly as decided — no rounding) |
| 2 | 3-7 | Email, same thread | 1-2 sentence resurface, nothing restated |
| 3 | 7-14 | LinkedIn DM | New proof point held back from touch 1, under ~300 characters, no link |
| 4 | 14-21 | Email | New value — seasonal hook, an easier CTA, or something new of theirs to reference |
| 5 (breakup) | 21-28 | Email | "Should I close your file?" — say it's the last email, then honor it |

Before any of this: tier the 180 (fit → traffic → existing monetization → already-promotes-a-competitor as a *positive* signal), run the manual brand-safety screen, and segment by jurisdiction — content-site editors skew heavily toward UK sole traders and solo operators generally, which get treated like Canada/Germany (consent required, no cold email) rather than blanket "UK is fine."

### Where the 12-attempt cadence still earns its keep

Only for a small, **named** tier-1 subset — and only if you carve out hours for it *separately* from your 4-hour weekly budget. The rule isn't "your best prospects deserve more attempts," it's "more attempts require standing hours you don't currently have." Without that separate budget, your tier-1 prospects run the same 5-touch sequence as everyone else — they just get it first and get the deepest personalization on touch 1.

**Bottom line:** the article isn't wrong that 12 attempts reaches more of the list eventually. It's answering a different question than the one you're actually facing. At 4 hours/week solo, the constraint is your time, not the size of the list — run 5 touches, work tiers in order, and only borrow the long cadence for a handful of prospects you're willing to fund with extra hours on top of the four.
