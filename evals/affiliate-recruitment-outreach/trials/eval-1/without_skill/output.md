## The legal split, in one line

**Cold email is a consent regime, and it's set per-recipient-type, not per-country-blanket.** Three of your four buckets are not homogeneous from a compliance standpoint — the US and Canada segments are, but UK and Germany aren't. That's the whole ballgame here.

| Segment                             | Count | Legal basis for cold email                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Verdict                                                   |
| ----------------------------------- | ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| US bloggers / comparison sites      | 88    | CAN-SPAM — opt-out regime, no prior consent needed for B2B or consumer email                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | **✅ Cold-emailable now**                                 |
| UK Ltd media companies              | 8     | PECR "corporate subscriber exemption" — unsolicited marketing to a company/LLP (generic or named-role inbox) is permitted without consent, opt-out required                                                                                                                                                                         | **✅ Cold-emailable now**                                 |
| UK solo newsletter/review operators | 46    | PECR treats sole traders / unincorporated one-person operations as **individual subscribers** — same rules as consumers, requires prior opt-in consent, no soft-opt-in available (no prior customer relationship)                                                                                                                   | **❌ Not cold-emailable**                                 |
| German B2B tech media sites         | 41    | UWG §7 — Germany requires prior explicit consent for commercial email even B2B, with essentially no cold-outreach exception; this is actively enforced via the German Abmahnung (cease-and-desist) industry                                                                                                                         | **❌ Not cold-emailable**                                 |
| Canadian independent consultants    | 27    | CASL — requires consent, but has an **implied-consent carve-out for conspicuous publication**: if the consultant has published their business email on their own site/LinkedIn in a professional capacity, without a "no unsolicited email" notice, and your message is relevant to their business function, implied consent exists | **⚠️ Cold-emailable, conditionally — verify per contact** |

**Net: 123 of 210 (59%) can be cold emailed. 87 of 210 (41%) cannot** — and Germany is the one where getting this wrong has real teeth (Abmahnung firms actively monitor B2B inboxes and unsolicited-email claims are a mature cottage industry there).

For the Canadian tier, before sending, screen each of the 27 for: email/contact form is publicly listed on their own consulting site (not scraped from a directory), no "do not solicit" disclaimer near it, and message ties to their stated practice area. Drop anyone who fails that filter into the Tier-2 motion below instead.

## Tier 1 — Cold email now (123 contacts)

Ship rules for all three: real physical mailing address in the footer, one-click unsubscribe honored within 10 business days, accurate from-name/subject, no purchased/scraped-in-bulk lists, and throttle sends (30–50/day per inbox, warmed domain) so you don't trip spam filters before you trip any regulator.

### A. US bloggers & comparison sites (88) — direct, numbers-first

Subject: `Adding Tessellate to your [analytics tools] roundup — 30% recurring`

> Hi [First name],
>
> I read your [specific post — e.g. "Best BI Tools for Startups 2026"] — solid rundown, and Tessellate isn't on it yet.
>
> We just opened our affiliate program: **30% recurring commission for 12 months, 90-day cookie, paid net-30.** No caps, no tiers to unlock — flat rate from referral #1.
>
> If you're open to it, I'll send over the product access, a comparison one-pager (feature/price table vs. [2 competitors likely in their post]), and unique tracking links — takes about 5 minutes to set up on your end.
>
> Worth a look for [post title]?
>
> [Name] · Tessellate
> [Physical address] · [Unsubscribe link]

Why this shape: comparison-site operators run on "vs." content and monetize via existing affiliate slots — lead with the commercial terms, not the product pitch, and hand them a ready-made asset (the comparison table) so accepting costs them no research time.

### B. UK Ltd media companies (8) — address the company, formal tone, name the exemption basis internally (not in the email)

Subject: `Partnership enquiry — Tessellate affiliate program`

> Dear [Company name] team,
>
> We're launching the affiliate program for Tessellate, a B2B analytics platform, and would like [Company] to be one of our UK launch partners.
>
> Terms: 30% recurring commission for 12 months, 90-day attribution window, net-30 payment.
>
> Could you point me to whoever handles partnership/affiliate relationships on your side? Happy to send full program details and a media kit.
>
> Best,
> [Name] · Tessellate
> [Physical address] · [Unsubscribe/opt-out link]

Send to a role-based or company inbox (partnerships@, editorial@) rather than a named individual's personal-looking address — that's what keeps you inside the corporate-subscriber exemption.

### C. Canadian independent consultants (27, screened) — warm, relevant, CASL footer mandatory

Subject: `A tool for your client recommendations — 30% recurring`

> Hi [First name],
>
> I came across your work on [specific: their site/LinkedIn content area — e.g. "ops dashboards for SMB clients"]. Tessellate is a B2B analytics tool a lot of consultants in that space end up recommending to clients.
>
> We just opened an affiliate program — 30% recurring for 12 months, 90-day cookie, net-30 — built for people like you who already influence tool decisions rather than run comparison blogs.
>
> Open to a quick look? Happy to set you up with a trial account first.
>
> [Name] · Tessellate
> [Full sender identity + physical/mailing address per CASL] · [Unsubscribe mechanism, functional for 60+ days]

CASL requires the message to identify the sender and any person on whose behalf it's sent, plus working contact info and an unsubscribe that's actioned within 10 business days — don't drop this footer even though the tone is casual.

## Tier 2 — No cold email (87 contacts): consent-first motion

Sending to these two groups cold is the one mistake that turns "affiliate launch" into a legal cleanup project — especially Germany, where competitor law firms actively scan for exactly this. Route them through channels that don't require prior email consent:

**1. Make them find you, not the reverse.** Stand up a public "Become a Tessellate Affiliate" landing page (terms, 30%/12mo/90-day/net-30, one-click apply) and get it indexed/shared where they already look — G2/Capterra affiliate listings, an affiliate marketplace (Awin and Impact both have strong UK/DACH reach), a LinkedIn/Xing post from your own account. This flips the interaction from marketing-to-them into them-opting-in, which sidesteps PECR and UWG entirely.

**2. LinkedIn (UK solo ops) / Xing or LinkedIn (Germany) outreach.** Platform DMs aren't governed by PECR/UWG email-marketing rules the same way email is — treat this as materially lower risk, not zero risk, and still respect platform ToS on outbound volume. A short, personal note inviting them to apply via the landing page works better here than a full pitch anyway, since these are individual operators who respond to being treated as a person, not a lead.

**3. Sponsor first, mention the program second.** For the 46 UK solo newsletters specifically, a genuine paid-sponsorship inquiry is a commercial negotiation you're initiating with them, not "direct marketing" sent to them — a materially different posture. Use that relationship, once it exists, as your opt-in path into the affiliate conversation. Don't use "sponsorship inquiry" as a pretext subject line for what's actually the affiliate pitch — that's the kind of dressed-up marketing email PECR is written to catch.

**4. For Germany specifically**, treat this as a PR/inbound problem, not an outreach problem: a submission to the trade sites' own "tools we use" or vendor-briefing intake (most German B2B tech media have one), a listing in DACH-market SaaS directories, or a paid placement. Any of those gets a human at the site to reach out to _you_ — at which point replying with the affiliate terms is a solicited response, fully clean.

## Net rollout

- **Send now:** 123 contacts (US 88, UK Ltd 8, Canada 27-screened) via Tier 1 copy above, throttled.
- **Build in parallel, no email:** affiliate landing page + Awin/Impact listing + LinkedIn/Xing presence, targeting the 87 in Tier 2 to self-opt-in.
- **Have counsel spot-check** the UK corporate-subscriber classification and the German consent posture before the first send — the cost of a 15-minute legal review is nothing next to a German Abmahnung letter or an ICO complaint two weeks into a launch.
