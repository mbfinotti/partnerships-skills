# Ranking your three moves (best → worst)

**1. Slack/Discord posting — keep, but fix the targeting**

- Zero cash cost, fastest to test.
- Real risk: "post the program" in _buyer_ communities reads as spam and gets you banned — buyers don't want to become affiliates, they want to evaluate a tool.
- Fix: target communities of potential _affiliates_ (ops/RevOps consultants, agencies who implement tools for clients, SaaS reviewers/bloggers), not buyer communities. Engage first, mention the program second.

**2. Coupon/cashback networks — deprioritize**

- Built for impulse ecommerce discount-hunting, not $49/seat recurring B2B software with a considered buying process.
- Public coupon codes undercut your premium positioning and let existing customers self-discount on renewal.
- These networks (Honey precedent) have known cookie-stuffing fraud — affiliates injecting their tracking cookie at checkout to steal commission from your real acquisition channels.
- Verdict: not worth the setup effort for this product.

**3. Buying a 5,000-contact list and blasting it — avoid as designed**

- Legal risk: cold-emailing a purchased list of individuals is on shaky ground under GDPR (you're in France — no clean legitimate-interest basis for a mass blast to people who never opted in).
- Deliverability risk: high spam-complaint rates from a cold blast can get your _sending domain_ blacklisted — hitting your sales and support email too, not just this campaign.
- Targeting mismatch: "marketing bloggers" ≠ people who write about workflow automation for ops teams. Weak affinity, weak conversion.
- If you want blogger outreach, do 50 hand-picked, personally-emailed bloggers who already write about ops/automation/SaaS comparisons — not 5,000 cold contacts.

# What's missing

**A real B2B affiliate infrastructure**

- Sign up with a B2B-native affiliate/partner platform instead of coupon networks: PartnerStack, Impact, Rewardful, or FirstPromoter. Built for recurring SaaS commissions, not one-time ecommerce discounts.

**The highest-LTV affiliate segment for a $49/seat recurring tool**

- Consultants and agencies who implement workflow tools for their clients. One agency partner can bring dozens of seats and stays an affiliate for years. This beats any coupon site or cold list by a wide margin.

**Review-site and comparison-page presence**

- G2, Capterra, TrustRadius listings + affiliate/referral programs there.
- Get listed on "best workflow automation tools" and "[competitor] alternatives" pages — these already rank and convert intent-driven traffic.

**Program economics and terms (you haven't defined these yet)**

- Cookie duration, minimum payout threshold, first-click vs last-click attribution.
- A no-brand-bidding clause (block affiliates from bidding on "Loomwright" in Google Ads — they'll cannibalize your own branded search otherwise).
- FTC-style affiliate disclosure requirement in your terms.

**Existing-customer referral loop**

- Two-sided incentive (discount for the referred customer + 20% for the affiliate) turns your own happy users into the cheapest, highest-trust acquisition channel — currently absent from your plan entirely.

**Affiliate enablement**

- A $49/seat B2B tool needs a demo environment, one-pagers, and talking points for affiliates to sell — unlike a coupon, it can't sell itself on a discount code alone.

**One question for you:** do you have a rough CAC/LTV target for this channel, or is Q4 explicitly an exploratory test budget? That changes how much I'd invest in platform fees (PartnerStack etc. run $500–1,500+/mo) vs. bootstrapping with Rewardful and manual outreach first.
