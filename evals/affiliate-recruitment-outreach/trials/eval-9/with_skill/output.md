## Ranking (least-bad → worst)

**1. Community posts (6 Slack/Discord groups) — keep, but reframe it**

The skill deletes "communities/forums" from the *active* outreach menu entirely, for both B2B and B2C — not demotes, deletes. Reason: in most of these spaces, anti-solicitation norms mean the cold pitch *is* the violation, regardless of how well you word it. "Post the program in six communities" as a broadcast move risks exactly that — six near-identical promo drops read as spam and can get the account banned or the brand talked about badly among the professional affiliates you actually want.

Fix, not cancel: check each community's self-promo rules first. Where allowed, participate and be findable — answer questions, mention the program when genuinely relevant, maybe one pinned/self-promo-day post — and treat anyone who bites as inbound. Don't run it as a 6-community blast on day one of Q4.

**2. Coupon/cashback networks — wrong category for this product, not wrong in general**

This is the one that surprised me: the skill explicitly deletes coupon/cashback/loyalty from the B2B SaaS sourcing menu — not because it's non-compliant, but because the value is negative. Discount-seeking traffic erodes recurring revenue, and cashback signups on a $49/seat/month tool are largely non-incremental (people who'd have bought anyway, now at a margin hit, forever, since your commission is recurring not one-time). That category earns its "high efficiency" ranking in B2C, where a one-off purchase discount makes sense — not here.

Signing up with three networks costs you almost nothing operationally, so I wouldn't call it harmful. But it shouldn't be one of three headline Q4 moves for a 20%-recurring B2B program — it's a low-priority, low-expectation side bet at best.

**3. Buying a 5,000-contact list and blasting it — don't do this one as described**

Two independent parts of the skill kill this outright:
- The compliance reference is explicit: *"Purchased consumer lists and scraped contact dumps of unknown provenance are not a workable basis; decline them and say why."*
- The failure-modes table lists "mass blast from one generic template" as the #1 documented way to burn a list — tiering and personalization is the whole point of the recruitment methodology this skill encodes.

Beyond the skill's own rules, the legal exposure is real and this is where it bites hardest: a vendor list of 5,000 bloggers will contain US, EU, UK sole-trader, and near-certainly Canadian and German contacts. Canada (CASL) and Germany (UWG) require a documented consent basis for cold email — a purchased list has none — and CASL penalties run up to CA$10M per violation, GDPR up to 4% of global turnover, CAN-SPAM up to ~$53k *per email* if the required identity/opt-out elements are missing (a vendor blast tool usually doesn't carry them correctly per-recipient). You'd also torch your sending domain's reputation for every future legitimate outreach email you send.

If there's a real list-building need here, buy or license a *backlink/publisher-discovery* tool instead (see below) — that's compliant and yields a qualified list, not a contact dump.

## What's actually missing

None of your three moves touch the categories this skill's B2B SaaS efficiency ranking actually leads with:

> `competitors' current affiliates > content/review sites > existing customers > newsletters > complementary vendors ≈ agencies/integration partners > YouTube/podcast`

For a $49/seat, 20%-recurring workflow-automation tool, in priority order:

1. **Competitors' current affiliates** — find who's already promoting your direct competitors (an hour of "[competitor] + affiliate program" search, or a day with a backlink tool) and pitch them a better deal. Proven category fit and monetization intent in one move — the highest-yield rung available and it's free to start.
2. **Content/review/comparison sites** — "best workflow automation software" / "best [category] for teams" listicles. Practitioners call this "the holy grail" for SaaS specifically because buyers research through these before they ever talk to sales.
3. **Existing happy customers** — near-zero cost to source (you already have the list), just needs a CS coordination pass to identify power users worth inviting.
4. **Newsletter operators** in the ops/no-code/automation space.

That's the actual Q4 plan. Concretely: run competitor-affiliate search + SERP mining this week (an afternoon), qualify and tier what comes back, draft a personalized 5-touch sequence (not a blast) per tier, and only then treat communities (passive) and coupon networks (minor side bet) as what they are — supplementary, not primary.

One thing I'd want from you before drafting any actual outreach copy: the cookie/attribution window and payout terms (you gave rate — 20% recurring — but not the window or payout cadence), and whether Q4 is a one-off push or a channel you're running long-term, since that changes how hard to lean into the slower, higher-value rungs (complementary vendors, integration partners) versus the fast ones above.
