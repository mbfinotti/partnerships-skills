**Verdict: your head of marketing is applying the wrong playbook to this audience. Partially right, mostly wrong.**

**Why "sell the brand, make them ask" is the wrong default here**

That rule is built for influencer/creator outreach, where curiosity and brand affinity drive opens and replies. Comparison-site editors are not that audience. They run "best contract review software" pages as a business — they get dozens of affiliate pitches a week and triage by economics, not vibes. For them:

- Commission rate is the qualifying filter, not the closer. No number → no reply, or a reply that just asks for the number, costing you a round-trip on 40 sends.
- They already know what Fernwick is, roughly, if they're worth pitching. Brand story doesn't do the work it does with a consumer creator.
- 30% recurring for 12 months is a genuinely strong offer in B2B SaaS affiliate terms — most run 20-25% one-time or a much shorter recurring window. Hiding it throws away your strongest asset. This is a number that should open doors, not one to bury.

**Where she's right**

Don't literally _lead_ with "30% commission!" as the subject line or first sentence — that reads like a mass affiliate-network blast and gets filtered as spam by editors who get exactly that pitch daily. Brand credibility (why Fernwick is worth a slot on their page — differentiation in contract review, traction, customer logos) should frame the email so it doesn't look like a generic reseller solicitation.

**What actually works for this send**

Don't choose between brand and terms — sequence them within the same email:

1. One line establishing why Fernwick is relevant to their audience (not a pitch, a credibility anchor).
2. A short, concrete partnership offer: 30% recurring / 12 months, 90-day cookie, net-45. Stated plainly, not hyped.
3. A specific, low-friction ask (a short call, or "reply and I'll send the affiliate agreement").

Withholding cookie length and payout terms too is worse than withholding commission alone — editors specifically screen for cookie window (90 days is good, say so) and payment terms (net-45 is average, not a selling point, but hiding it invites suspicion more than disclosing it).

**One caveat**: if any of these 40 are hybrid consumer/business publications where the page format is more "reviews" than "comparison tables," a soft version of her approach (brand-first, terms in a clear P.S. or linked one-pager rather than inline) is reasonable. But as a blanket rule for comparison-site editors, "never lead with money" will suppress your reply rate on exactly the recipients who decide fastest when they see the number.

**Bottom line**: reframe the debate from _brand vs. money_ to _sequencing_ — brand context first, real terms in the same email, no forced follow-up just to get basic facts a professional affiliate needs to say yes.
