## Your CMO is half right — separate the diagnosis from the prescription

The list isn't tainted. Recruiting affiliates who already monetize your category is standard partner-marketing practice — no different from bidding on competitor keywords or reading their pricing page. It only becomes "poaching" if you frame the outreach as _"drop them, switch to us."_ Frame it as _"add us as an option"_ and it's just... affiliate recruitment. Do both things your CMO wants: use the list, and don't look desperate. They aren't mutually exclusive.

## Step 1 — Don't treat 63 sites as one list

Split by site type, because the right approach differs completely:

- **"Best scheduling tool" / comparison / listicle sites** — highest value. They're built to add options, not defend one vendor. Low-friction ask.
- **Coupon/deal aggregators** — just apply through your own affiliate program signup like any other applicant. No personalized pitch needed, no "poaching" optics at all.
- **Niche B2B/SaaS blogs and newsletters** — real editorial relationships. Worth a real pitch, but a slow one.
- **Directories/marketplaces (Capterra-style, niche software directories)** — check if you're already listed; if not, that's a listing gap, not an affiliate pitch.
- **Low-quality/PBN-looking sites** — filter these out entirely (check DR, traffic, and whether the "review" content reads like templated spam). Recruiting them adds noise and refund/chargeback risk, not revenue.

Pull DR and estimated traffic per domain (Ahrefs) before prioritizing — rank the list by traffic × topical relevance, not by count.

## Step 2 — Kill the "poaching" framing at the outreach level

Never reference the competitor in outreach. Never say "I noticed you promote X." That's the part that reads as desperate and could burn a relationship you might want later (these sites may cover you organically someday).

Use instead:

- "We're building our affiliate program for B2B scheduling tools and your [post/site] is exactly the audience we want in front of."
- Lead with your program terms, since they're genuinely strong: **25% recurring for 12 months + 60-day cookie** beats most SaaS programs (typical range is 20-30% for month one only, rarely 12 months recurring). Let the terms do the persuading, not the competitive angle.
- For listicle/comparison sites specifically: offer to supply a comparison blurb, screenshots, and a unique tracking link — reduce their effort to add you to zero.

## Step 3 — Respect program exclusivity where it exists

Some affiliate networks have exclusivity clauses in their terms (rare in B2B SaaS, more common in finance/insurance affiliate niches, but check). A handful of the 63 may be contractually unable to run both. Don't let this stop outreach — just don't be surprised by declines, and don't argue with a "we have an exclusivity agreement" reply.

## Step 4 — Build the CMO's "clean list" in parallel, not instead

Source it differently so it's genuinely additive:

- Query affiliate marketplaces (Impact, PartnerStack, Rewardful marketplace) for "scheduling," "calendar," "appointment booking" categories — sites that haven't picked a side yet.
- Search "[competitor alternative]" and "best appointment scheduling software" SERPs for sites _not_ in your 63 — these are unclaimed territory.
- Target B2B SaaS newsletter/podcast operators via a tool like Sparktoro for audience overlap with your ICP (ops managers, agencies, coaches, clinics — whoever your actual buyer is).

Run both lists through the same program terms and tracking setup. Report them separately in your affiliate dashboard so you can see conversion rate by list — that answers, empirically, whether "already-primed" affiliates convert better than "cold" ones, which is useful data for the next round.

## Step 5 — Sequence it

1. This week: apply to the 15-20 coupon/deal aggregators directly (no personalization needed, near-zero cost).
2. Next 2 weeks: personalized outreach to the top 10-15 comparison/listicle sites by traffic, using the "add us" framing.
3. Ongoing: slower relationship-building outreach to the handful of real editorial blogs — these convert on trust, not on a first cold email.
4. Parallel: CMO's clean-list build, same cadence.

## The actual answer to "what do I do with this list"

Use it as a **prioritized affiliate recruitment target list**, not a competitive attack list. Segment by site type, strip the low-quality ones, lead every pitch with your program terms instead of the competitor's name, and run it alongside — not instead of — a freshly-sourced list. That gets you the upside your CMO is worried you'd lose (the optics) without leaving 63 pre-qualified, proven-to-convert-in-category partners on the table.
