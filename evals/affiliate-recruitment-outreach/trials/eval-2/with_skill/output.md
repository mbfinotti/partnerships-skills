# What to do with the 63-site list

Your CMO is describing a real risk with the wrong label. Reaching out to sites that already run affiliate links for competitors is not "poaching" — it's the single highest-yield sourcing method in affiliate recruitment (Prussakov calls it the #1 method: "Identify the affiliates who make your direct competitors successful... then reach out"). These 63 sites have already proven three things a clean list hasn't: audience fit for scheduling-tool buyers, a working monetization setup, and intent to run affiliate content. Discarding that and starting cold is the more expensive path, not the safer one.

What actually creates the "desperate/poaching" feel isn't the list — it's bad execution on it: mass-blasting one template, leading with commission %, disparaging the competitor, or promising lax disclosure. None of that is required to work this list well. Fix the execution, keep the list.

The real risk in your CMO's objection isn't ethics, it's contract: some of these 63 may sit under network exclusivity or non-solicitation terms tied to the competitor's program. That's a screening step, not a reason to abandon the list — see the screen below.

Recommendation: run this list as your **tier-1 opening wave**, in parallel with (not instead of) building the "clean" list from SERP-mined content sites and newsletter operators — that's your tier-2/3 pipeline once tier 1 is worked. You get the fast, high-conversion wins now and the broader base later.

## RECRUITMENT OUTREACH PLAN — Kettleback affiliate recruitment, 2026-09-12

### Prospect tiers

- **Tier 1 (default: all 63, pending the screen below)** — sourced via competitor-affiliate link-intersect, the highest-efficiency and highest-compliance-cost category simultaneously for B2B SaaS. Real traffic and proven monetization are already evidenced by the live affiliate tracking parameters — you don't need to re-verify authority, only audience fit and safety.
- Before treating all 63 as tier 1: individual research runs ~1 day/prospect at true tier-1 depth. 63 days of manager time isn't realistic for one launch. **Split after the screen**: sites whose content directly targets scheduling-tool buyers (comparison/review posts, "best tools for X" roundups) stay tier 1 and get individual personalization; sites with only tangential fit (general SaaS directories, broad business blogs) drop to tier 2 and run the standard sequence without per-prospect research.

### Required screen before any outreach (do this to the whole 63 first)

1. **Brand safety** — drop or flag: trademark bidding on "Kettleback," coupon-injection/adware behavior, cookie-stuffing patterns, thin/scraped content. Check what the affiliate tracking parameters actually resolve to — if any route through a coupon/cashback network, treat that subset as B2C-shaped traffic (deleted category for B2B SaaS: it's non-incremental here) and drop it rather than pitch it.
2. **Contract exposure** — for any site clearly run by a managed network program, check whether that network's terms carry a non-solicitation clause before the first send. Never ask a prospect to break an existing agreement; drop rather than pitch if unclear.
3. **Audience fit** — does the content target the people who buy a B2B scheduling tool (ops/agency/SMB buyers), or just contain an incidental competitor mention? Filters your true tier 1 from the rest.

### Legal segments — the one thing I can't fill in for you

I don't have the 63 domains' locations. **This has to happen before any email goes out** — the regime attaches to the prospect's location, not yours:

- US and most EU prospects → standard cold sequence, CAN-SPAM/GDPR must-carry items (identity, physical postal address, working opt-out).
- Germany → opt-in required, treat like Canada — no cold email without a documented consent basis.
- UK → check legal form first: Ltd/LLP/Scottish partnership/public body → cold sequence OK. Sole trader or personal/freemail address (the norm for comparison-site editors and bloggers — this is most of your list's shape) → consent basis required, same as Canada.
- Canada → no cold email without express/implied consent (existing relationship, or a published role-relevant contact address you can document).

Practical move: pull each domain's About/footer or a quick Companies House check, bucket the 63 into "cold-eligible" vs "consent-required," and run only the passive route (program page, directory listing, no cold pitch) on the second bucket.

### Chosen angle

Three candidates:

1. **"You already prove this converts" (recommended)** — cites their existing competitor coverage directly and plainly as the reason you're reaching out, per Raschella: "you find the people already selling products like yours and offer them a better deal." Turns the CMO's exact worry into the pitch's honesty, rather than hiding it.
2. **Pure content-gap angle** ("your roundup is missing a tool your readers ask about") — softer, doesn't name the competitor coverage. Weaker: less specific, reads more like a template.
3. **Referral-by-name** — only usable if you have a real mutual contact per prospect; with 63 cold sites, this rung is mostly unavailable. Don't fabricate one.

Angle 1 wins: it's the most specific (per-prospect verifiable fact), it's the one the qualification data already supports, and it neutralizes the "poaching" feel by being upfront rather than evasive about why you found them.

### Sequence (5-touch default, ~4 weeks)

| Touch | Day | Channel | Angle | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Email | Fit + economics | Template above |
| 2 | 3–7 | Email, same thread | Resurface | "Did this reach you? Happy to answer anything." — 1-2 sentences, nothing restated |
| 3 | 7–14 | LinkedIn DM | Proof | One conversion stat withheld from touch 1, <300 chars, no link |
| 4 | 14–21 | Email | Ease | Pre-filled application + a ready-made comparison one-pager for their next roundup update |
| 5 | 21–28 | Email | Breakup | "Planning to close your file — if the timing's off, say the word and I'll circle back next quarter." Then honor it. |

Stop immediately on reply, opt-out, or bounce, on every touch.

### Tracking plan

- Pipeline stages: contacted → replied → applied → activated (first click), in a CRM/spreadsheet.
- Review weekly during the campaign.
- KPIs: reply rate per tier (target 3–9%, cold-email norm; expect upside from this list's high fit), application rate, and the metric that actually matters — activation rate (recruits producing ≥1 click/week). Approvals without activation cost you outreach and return nothing.
